# Progression, Items, and Rival Waves SPEC

This specification extends `specs/SPEC.md` and `specs/MECHANICS_SPEC.md` and is authoritative for progression and itemization.

Selling equipment returns Gold equal to ten times the item's base `sellValue` in every mode, including the Halls of Realms lobby, unmatched play, and competitive realms. This multiplier applies only to direct sale proceeds; upgrade prices, extraction prices, enemy bounty, and other base-value calculations remain unchanged. Inventory labels and previews show the multiplied proceeds.

## Optional Loadouts and Spell Binding

Hero, public-profile, rival-clone, and death-echo main-hand slots may be empty. Heroes and copied player builds have four independent equipment slots: main hand, offhand, amulet, and charm. Equipping an equipped item toggles it off without creating or restoring a Plain Club; the backpack stack retains the equipped copy. Offhands, amulets, and charms may be equipped while empty-handed. Equipping a two-handed weapon still displaces an offhand but never displaces the amulet or charm. Generated ordinary enemies remain weapon-equipped.

Healing is the only spell globally bound to a newly created hero. Extraction upgrades only spells the hero already permanently owns; equipment carrying any unlearned extractable spell cannot be extracted. Epic extraction of an already learned spell additionally binds it globally, allowing it to cast without matching equipment. Reactive Blocking is not extractable.

## Progression

- Heroes begin at level 0 with zero Agility, Strength, Magic, Spirit, and Intelligence, 10 base maximum HP, 5 base maximum mana, empty main-hand, offhand, amulet, and charm slots, exactly three random level-0 Common backpack items, and Healing globally bound at level 1.
- Progression uses a quadratic cumulative curve: total XP required to reach level `L` is `15 * L²`. The incremental cost from level `L` to `L + 1` is therefore `15 * (2L + 1)`, producing `15, 45, 75, 105, 135, 165, ...`. Level lookup uses the inverse square-root formula with non-negative integer clamping. Persisted profiles retain their earned level by raising stored XP to at least `15 * savedLevel²` during migration. Each level grants five integer attribute points using editable non-negative weights totaling 5. Allocation uses one minus button, scalar value, and plus button per stat rather than raw number fields. Buttons clamp values to zero through the shared five-point budget; the panel shows used and remaining budget. Reset sets the pending allocation to all zero without saving. Save is enabled only at exactly five points, and the server independently validates non-negative integers totaling 5. Hovering or focusing the panel replaces each displayed current attribute with its projected next-level value and updates that preview as controls change; only values that actually increase are green, and no separate `+N` label is shown. A separate retroactive ratio reset submits the currently displayed valid five-point allocation, costs `100 * currentLevel` Gold, saves that allocation for future levels, and replaces every base stat with `allocationForStat * currentLevel`; insufficient Gold rejects the reset without mutation.
- Maximum HP and mana are derived rather than persisted: `maxHP = 10 + Strength` and `maxMana = 5 + 2 * Intelligence`. Existing heroes therefore receive current resource maxima automatically from their stored attributes after load. Progression updates during an active wave, including enemy kills and currency or item collection, preserve current mana and stamina rather than refilling them; each is only capped if its recalculated maximum decreases. Explicit realm and defeat resets still restore resources. Strength also adds 0.2 base damage and 1 stamina per point. Agility contributes to one-handed handling and adds 1% critical chance. Magic remains an allocated attribute for spell-oriented progression without directly increasing the resource pool. Natural health regeneration is deliberately weak at `0.005 + 0.005 * Spirit` HP/second. Mana regeneration is `0.2 + 0.1 * Spirit` per second, while stamina regenerates four times more slowly at `0.05 + 0.025 * Spirit` per second. Intelligence also adds 5% critical damage, 1% cooldown reduction, and 2% magic amplification.
- Critical chance is capped at 75%, cooldown reduction at 60%, and base critical damage is 150%.
- Defeat outside Training resets cumulative XP, level, and earned base attributes to zero. Inventory, allocation, scraps, learned skills, their durable spell-priority order, and currently equipped items persist. The defeated hero loses `floor(Gold / 2)` Gold and `floor(Souls / 2)` Souls; an attributable opposing-player killer receives those exact amounts, otherwise they are lost.
- Voluntary defeat uses the same progression and currency reset and is allowed in Training Grounds. Every combat or voluntary defeat snapshots the pre-reset level, base stats, main hand, offhand, amulet, and charm into one hostile rival echo queued for the highest-ranked durable hero when the defeated hero is not that recipient. The recipient is selected by level descending then username ascending and receives the echo in their next authored wave.
- XP, attributes, allocation, gold, souls, scraps, learned skills, learned skill levels, equipment, backpack stacks, and quantities survive defeat, username login, opaque-id reconnection, and server restarts through the Bun SQL hero blob.
- Souls are a persistent currency awarded one at a time for credited realm/player kills. The fixed header at the top of the right inventory column displays Gold, Souls, and the four rarity scrap balances in a six-cell grid that remains visible during enemy inspection and while the equipment list scrolls. Clicking Uncommon, Rare, or Epic Scrap promotes 100 of its immediately lower rarity into one of the clicked rarity; Common has no lower rarity and is not promotable. Shift-click promotes every complete batch available at once. Promotion is server-authoritative, does not use Gold or Souls, and a hover/focus preview shows the single-batch Scrap cost and gain in the same header cells.
- Beneath the five allocated attributes, the character side panel places Next-level allocation immediately above Effective stats. It is collapsed by default to an unoutlined text label with no arrow indicator. Hovering it or focusing within it reveals its controls as a bordered panel and activates projected attribute/effective-stat differences; leaving collapses it again unless focus remains within. The effective-stat sheet is produced by one pure calculation from base attributes, main hand, and offhand, allowing the UI to evaluate current or mocked builds without mutating the hero. It separates the same complete comparison into Offensive, Defensive, and Utility sections. The values include weapon damage and attacks/second, attack resource cost, critical chance and multiplier, magic amplification, cooldown reduction, per-skill-level Spirit range gain, maximum health/stamina/mana, health/mana/stamina regeneration, Defense, block chance/cost, life steal, status chances, and Attraction. Hovering an inventory item recalculates it as an equip replacement: the current item in that slot is removed first, and a two-handed preview also removes the offhand. Changed effective values are green until preview ends. `Defense` is the current buckler mitigation cap (`Strength`) and is zero without a buckler; this label does not imply a separate always-on armor system. Zero values remain visible in this character summary so the full build can be compared at a glance.
- Hover-difference previews are presentation-only and never mutate authoritative progression. Allocation hover/focus and inventory-card equip previews project changed attributes and effective stats in green directly in the left character sheet. Every action-button resource projection replaces the affected Gold, Soul, or rarity-Scrap value in the fixed inventory header with only its projected total, using green for gains and red for costs. All six currency cells keep their labels and values on one line and retain a stable height while preview text changes. Extract hover additionally projects each affected spell level in green directly in the bottom-left spell rail and highlights every extractable skill row on the source item in blue; non-extractable Blocking remains unchanged. Sell previews one copy's Gold gain, Purge previews one copy's matching-rarity Scrap gain, Upgrade previews one copy's Gold and matching-rarity Scrap costs plus the level+1 item result directly on the hovered inventory card, and Extract previews its Gold cost plus one permanent level for every carried, already learned extractable skill. Item-tile projections replace level, attack values, effects, requirements, and every other changed tile field with only the projected value while retaining green or red change coloring. Effects and Skills each render as a compact, unbulleted vertical list with one stable row per entry and no list indentation; Upgrade changes each row's value and color in place without changing the list's row count or tile height. Hovering or keyboard-focusing an individual skill row shows that skill's authored skillbar description and stat-bonus description in a styled tooltip. Other tile labels and values remain single-line, clipping overflow with an ellipsis so previewing never changes card height. Item names, Requirements, and individually truncated effect rows expose their complete current or projected text in a styled tooltip on pointer hover or keyboard focus. Requirements are projected per attribute; any upgraded requirement above the hero's current matching stat is red, while changed requirements that remain met are green. When the action upgrades the equipped copy, the same projected item also updates the equipped card and character stats in the left sheet. When an equipped stack has spare copies, Upgrade previews the spare-copy item result on the inventory card without changing the character sheet because the server upgrades spares first. Upgrade and Extract Gold projections may become negative to communicate shortages. Button hover previews always describe one action even though Shift+click may request a bulk loop. Progress updates, including pickups and enemy defeats, preserve and recompute the active card or action-button preview when its keyed card still exists beneath the pointer. Leaving the button or card restores authoritative displayed balances, spells, equipment, and stats; no separate diff panel appears beneath inventory actions.
- Hovering an inventory card previews its Equip or Unequip result using the same distributed preview: base/equipment-adjusted attributes and advanced combat stats update in the character sheet, while skills gained, lost, or level-changed update in the spell rail. Left-clicking the card outside its action controls performs that equip toggle; there is no separate Equip or Unequip button. Every previewable UI value is prepared as `{ currentVal, newVal }`, where `newVal` may be `null` for removal. Shared formatting always renders only the projected value, or `—` for a removal; previews never include before-and-after text. Extract spell-level previews retain their full capped/effective projected number when present (for example, `5/19`). Shared tone utilities apply green to improvements/gains and red to regressions/costs. Feature code supplies data rather than hand-formatting preview strings separately for resources, stats, advanced stats, or spells.

## Items and Skills

Per-spell behavior is specified in `specs/spells/<skill-id>.md`. These files own a spell's identity, source, activation, cost, targeting, effect, scaling, status behavior, and presentation. This file owns the shared extraction, effective-level, cooldown, range, and HUD rules that those contracts reference. Item-applied slow effects have one file per effect in `specs/item-effects/`; there are currently no such effects. Do not add an item slow without first adding its contract there. Attack wind-up/recovery slow and Freeze are simulation mechanics, not item slows, and remain in `specs/MECHANICS_SPEC.md`.

- Every generated item rolls defensive perks from Defense, Physical Resist, Magic Resist, Fire Resist, Frost Resist, Poison Resist, Bleed Resist, and Dodge. Common, Uncommon, Rare, and Epic items carry one, two, three, and four distinct parallel perks respectively. Item level supplies perk magnitude, with each perk rolling within its level-scaled range and reaching its declared maximum only at level 50; rarity increases breadth, not the maximum of one perk. Beginning at generated item level 25, each rolled resistance perk has an independent 10% chance to become a matching true immunity instead of ordinary resistance. Immunity is a distinct preserved item trait, is shown as `Immune to <type>`, and operates only while that item's requirements are fully met. Dodge is capped at 50%, each non-immune resistance at 50%, and Defense is flat damage reduction applied before percentage resistance. Physical and Bleed use Agility/Strength requirements, Magic and elemental resistance use Intelligence (Fire also Magic), Poison uses Spirit/Agility, Defense uses Strength, and Dodge uses Agility. Perk-derived requirements use five times item level distributed over their thematic attributes. Ordinary level upgrades retain perk and immunity identities and values; rarity promotion adds newly random distinct perk types until the new rarity count is met.

- Hardcoded item definitions and randomized generators live in a shared configuration module. Generator inputs are level, rarity, class filters, and a deterministic seed; generated instances contain no functions.
- Weapon classes are club, sword, dagger, mace, axe, one-handed throwing axe, one-handed hammer, and staff. Rare and Epic maces additionally carry Healing; equipping one contributes its normal temporary skill level, while extracting a spare can permanently upgrade Healing. Newly created players equip a deterministic level-0 Common Throwing Axe and a deterministic level-0 Common Buckler, with one reserved copy of each in the backpack; this default does not rewrite existing saved equipment. Throwing axes make physical projectile basic attacks at a short 210-pixel range and have an inherent 15% chance to bleed on every direct hit. The plain starter club remains the zero-value fallback restored when a main hand is unequipped.
- Edged weapons may bleed; mace/club weapons may stun; venomous and rusty affixes may poison compatible weapons; staves may amplify magic and mana regeneration. Throwing axes may also roll the Bleeding, Rusty, Venomous, and Swift affixes.
- Requirements, damage, speed, affixes, skills, stat bonuses, modifiers, drop chance, and sell value scale with item level and rarity. Every generated equipment item has at least one positive direct attribute bonus. Generated items use a deliberately sparse drop chance of `min(30%, 4% + 6% * rarityPower)`, currently about 10% for common through 16.6% for epic gear.
- Requirements never prevent equipping or retaining an item. For every unmet requirement, let `delta = requiredStat - heroStat`; the item's positive stats and effects are divided by `delta + 1`. Divisors from multiple unmet requirements multiply. This effectiveness applies to weapon output and affixes, item attribute bonuses, defensive perks, block chance and reflection, sustain, attraction, Gold gain, and rarity boost, while costs and requirements themselves are not reduced. Requirement penalties cannot reduce weapon attack below that same weapon's level-0 attack value: the floor preserves its class, rarity, affixes, and current wielder context while removing only `weaponLevelScale(currentLevel)`. They also cannot reduce attack speed below the weapon's ordinary weight-and-handling speed; a positive attack-speed modifier may be reduced toward that floor but never below it. Unmet requirements render red and show the resulting lost-output percentage as an `X% penalty to item stats` message. By default, the card shows the item's affected values without the requirement penalty. Hovering or focusing the penalty message projects those values to the hero's actual current penalized output, with losses highlighted red; costs and requirements remain unchanged, and exit restores the unpenalized values. Equip and allocation previews use the same calculation.
- Weapon attack timing is weight-based instead of using a global linear Agility multiplier. The handling stat is Agility for one-handed physical weapons, Strength for two-handed physical weapons, and the average of Strength and Spirit for magic/staff weapons. Base attack interval in seconds is `weight / (10 + 0.1 * handlingStat)`; displayed attacks per second is its reciprocal multiplied by item attack-speed modifiers such as Swift. Canonical weights are Dagger 8, Club 12, Sword 14, Staff 16, and Mace 18. This formula is shared by the HUD, hero combat, and generated enemies.
- Clubs, swords, daggers, maces, axes, throwing axes, and hammers require one hand; staves require two. A one-handed build may equip one buckler or relic in its offhand. Amulets and charms each have their own independent equipment slot; neither occupies or displaces the offhand or the other accessory slot. Relics do not block. Some generated staves, relics, amulets, and charms carry Attraction, a passive that pulls all uncollected arena item drops toward the wearer at 35 pixels/second. Attraction does not stack between equipped items. Equipped items remain as reserved copies in their original inventory stacks and count toward occupied capacity. Equipping from an `xN` stack neither decrements nor splits that stack: the card remains `xN`, exactly one copy is reserved as equipped, and the other `N - 1` copies remain available for inventory actions. Equipping another item does not consume its tile; any previously equipped item is also retained as an inventory copy. An equip is rejected if retaining equipment that predates the inventory would exceed capacity.
- Amulets and charms are level-based accessories. Amulets equip only in the dedicated amulet slot and charms equip only in the dedicated charm slot; neither occupies or conflicts with the offhand, a two-handed weapon, or the other accessory. Both follow the existing reserve, upgrade, preview, and requirement-penalty rules, with replacement previews displacing only equipment in the same slot. Their number of distinct accessory rolls is chosen within the rarity band: Common 1–2, Uncommon 1–3, Rare 2–4, and Epic 4–6. Amulets favor attribute bonuses, +1–5 levels to mana-resource skills, +1–3 levels to every available skill, +1–10 levels to stamina-resource skills, automatic item pull, 5–80% global cooldown reduction, 5–90% mana- or life-cost reduction, and the extractable Time Harvest passive. Time Harvest removes 1 second from every hero cooldown after each enemy kill at level 1, scaling linearly with its effective skill level to a 10-second removal at level 99; its equipped temporary level scales linearly from 1 at amulet level 0 to 99 at amulet level 50, while extracted permanent levels use the normal skill progression. It affects attack, active-skill, Healing, Blocking, and aura-pulse cooldowns. Item pull uses the normal Attraction movement behavior without granting an extractable spell. Global cooldown reduction from all equipped items stacks with Intelligence but the final effective reduction remains capped at 80%; mana and life reductions from all equipped items stack after the base numeric or percentage cost and are each capped at 90%. Charms favor those cost reductions plus bonus damage applied to every successful physical basic or skill hit; each rolled Frost, Poison, Bleed, Fire, or Lightning bonus contributes a flat percentage of that hit's post-mitigation physical damage as a separate magic hit. Accessory skill levels are temporary, requirement-scaled, capped by the normal level-99 effective skill limit, and are shown in spell-rail equip previews.
- Inventory capacity is `10 + round(playerLevel / 10)` occupied equipment stacks. Exact matching equipment shares quantity. Empty, unequipped stacks are removed. A new unmatched stored drop remains on the ground when all occupied slots are full.
- When defeat resets player level and therefore lowers inventory capacity, equipped main-hand and offhand stacks are always protected. If the backpack is over its new capacity, the newest wholly unequipped stacks are removed first and every copy becomes a server-owned equipment ground drop; equipped stacks and their reserved copies are never selected for overflow removal.
- Inventory presentation never renders zero-quantity stacks, even if stale state arrives during migration. It preserves acquisition order for every stack regardless of equipped state, with the latest acquisition at the bottom. The inventory drawer is twice the 320px character-panel width and lays backpack cards out as a two-column grid.
- Matching includes kind, class, level, rarity, hands, affixes, requirements, bonuses, modifiers, skills, stamina cost, and reflection components while ignoring instance id, seed, generated name, drop chance, and sell value.
- Selling, purging, extracting, sending, and upgrading normally affect one copy. Holding Shift while clicking any of those action buttons sends a bulk request; Sell, Purge, Extract, and Send consume every available spare copy in the clicked stack, multiplying their currency, scrap, skill-level, or queue result per copy, while Upgrade repeats until copies or resources run out. The server stops immediately when an iteration makes no progress. Equipped copies remain reserved from destructive actions, but an equipped stack keeps Sell, Purge, Send, and Extract enabled whenever its quantity exceeds its equipped-copy count; those actions consume only spare copies and stop before the equipped copy. Upgrade is disabled only when Gold or matching-rarity Scrap is insufficient: it upgrades spare copies first, then upgrades the equipped copy in place while preserving its equipped slot. Purging grants `max(1, ceil(itemLevel / 3))` scraps of the item's rarity.
- Upgrading consumes one copy with lower base costs that rise with direct item attributes. Let `attributePoints` be the sum of positive values in `item.statBonuses` and `attributeFactor = 1 + 0.1 * attributePoints`; Gold cost is `ceil(1.5 * sellValue * attributeFactor)` and same-rarity Scrap cost is `ceil(2 * (currentLevel + 1) * attributeFactor)`. It creates one level+1 configuration preserving class, rarity, affixes, skills, rolled perk identities and values, accessory bonuses, sustain identity, attraction, reflection components, and the set of required attributes while recalculating level-derived core values. Every successful upgrade, including a rarity promotion, increases one of the item's existing positive direct attribute bonuses by exactly one; a legacy item with no direct attribute bonus first gains one point in a deterministic attribute. Existing requirements may increase with the new level but never disappear or switch to different attributes. No duplicate equipment copy is required. When the consumed copy is equipped, its upgraded result immediately replaces it in the same equipment slot. The output requires a matching tile or free permanent tile. A Shift+click upgrade repeats upgrades from the clicked source stack while its current copies and resources permit; it does not create persistent automation or process future pickups.
- Equipment level caps are Common 10, Uncommon 15, Rare 30, and Epic 50. Generated equipment is clamped to its rarity cap. Upgrading a max-level Common, Uncommon, or Rare item promotes it to the next rarity and resets its level to 1 while preserving its equipment class and the complete rolled-trait configuration defined above; the promoted rarity recalculates rarity-derived core power, block values, drop value, and sell value without rerolling requirements or effects. Epic level 50 is final and cannot be upgraded. Promotion uses the normal Upgrade action, costs based on the pre-promotion item, and participates in the same hover projection and Shift+click loop.
- Weapon level uses a primary multiplier of `1 + 0.025 * weaponLevel`. It scales weapon damage, stamina cost, item-granted critical/bleed/poison/stun chances, life-steal base, and the Strength coefficient of Vigorous regeneration. The equipped main hand also applies the smaller skill multiplier `1 + 0.005 * weaponLevel`: effective skill range is multiplied by it and effective skill cooldown is divided by it. These level multipliers stack with rarity, affixes, character stats, permanent skill levels, and cooldown reduction. Attack speed, weight, affix identity, and skill level are not directly increased by weapon level.
- Sending consumes one exact item and queues it for realm-wave use. Each sent item also enqueues one XP-send buff for the sender. Buffs run FIFO and never overlap: the first begins immediately, and each later buff begins only after its predecessor expires. Every buff lasts `10 * itemLevel` seconds and multiplies normal earned XP by the sent item's rarity: Common 120%, Uncommon 150%, Rare 200%, and Epic 300%, rounded down to whole XP. The server owns the queue and timer and applies the multiplier after the active balance-profile and solo-wave modifiers. The current buff appears as a positive hero status above the Mana bar, with its multiplier and remaining time; queued buffs are not displayed until they become current. If an equip swap cannot store old equipment, the old equipment becomes a server-confirmed ground drop.
- A creep equipped with a player-sent item has a visible outline matching that exact item's rarity color. The outline identifies player equipment regardless of whether the sent item replaced main hand or offhand; generated and merely carried equipment does not add this outline.
- All spells store up to level 99, but their active effective level is `min(actualSkillLevel, heroLevel)`. Skills carried by equipped equipment contribute one temporary actual skill level before that hero-level cap. An unknown equipped skill is therefore available at actual level 1; if it is already permanently learned, equipping the item raises its actual level by one until unequipped without mutating permanent progression. The Learned Skills rail contains every permanently learned skill, and the Geared Skills rail contains every equipped skill that is not permanently learned. Every displayed slot is enabled by default; clicking it toggles that individual skill. Enabled slots execute, while disabled slots remain visible but dark gray and never trigger. Skills outside both rails are inactive. There is no level-based active-skill limit or hidden active reserve. Automatic casts use the stable rail order and rotate after each successful cast so a short-cooldown skill cannot starve another; toggling never reorders skills. Backpack equipment with at least one extractable skill exposes an Extract action, but its button is disabled unless every carried extractable skill is already permanently learned. Blocking is an extractable buckler passive like other item skills. Extraction is server-owned, consumes that item, and permanently adds one actual level to each carried extractable skill up to level 99. Its Gold cost is `10 * currentPermanentLevel` per extracted skill, summed across all carried skills; it therefore depends on the skills being upgraded rather than the item's rarity or sell value. Epic universal-unlock metadata remains durable for existing heroes, but learned skills are active regardless of the source equipment. Healing and Rent are learned defaults. Effective spell levels scale at least one meaningful parameter such as damage, healing, or cooldown.
- Every weapon, including common drops and the starter club, carries its class signature skill: clubs use Bash, swords Sweep, daggers Flurry, maces Shockwave, axes Cleave, throwing axes Rending Throw, hammers Orbiting Hammers, and staves Arcane Bolt. Cleave is a broad physical axe strike. Rending Throw is a short-ranged physical projectile that guarantees a standard bleed on hit. Orbiting Hammers costs 3 mana and automatically launches three magical hammer projectiles at evenly spaced angles; each spirals outward around the moving hero, damages the first enemy it intersects, and remains active for a duration that scales linearly from 2.4 seconds at level 1 to 30 seconds at level 99. Shockwave is a full-circle physical impact with its own cooldown, damage multiplier, range, stun, and canvas effect; it is not an alias of Bash.
- Attraction Idols carry extractable passive Attraction as well as the extractable Force Field skill (retaining the stable `gravityPull` protocol/content id). Attraction pulls all uncollected arena item drops toward the wearer at 35 pixels/second, remains subject to the source Idol's requirement effectiveness, does not stack, and can be toggled from the spell rail. Epic extraction makes Attraction universal at the same rate without requiring an equipped Idol. Force Field costs 8 mana, has an 18-second base cooldown, and has a 200–800px level-scaled radius. It deals up to `0.6 * spellPower(level)` magic damage and replaces velocity with up to a 180px/s outward launch; both diminish linearly to zero at that radius. Force Field never moves uncollected drops of any kind. Rare and epic spiked bucklers carry two additional extractable skills: Thorns is passive and returns 5% of incoming direct damage even when an attack is not blocked; Reflective Surge is a reactive skill that spends 3 stamina only when its bearer is hit while it is enabled and ready, applies to that triggering hit, then for 6 seconds doubles Thorns and successful-block reflection while adding another 1% of incoming damage to every return. Its base cooldown is 16 seconds, and it never activates merely because an enemy is in range. Rare and epic staves additionally carry Frozen Orb. Frozen Orb costs 10 mana, has a 20-second base cooldown, launches a slow moving orb that periodically emits damaging radial ice spikes, and applies Freeze from the orb and its spikes.
- Idol perk rolls can grant either of two additional extractable passives. Spirit Wounds is an upkeep-free aura that triggers whenever any bearer-authored damage critically strikes, including attacks, skills, projectiles, auras, reflection, accessory damage, statuses, and continuous damage. It restores Mana equal to 1% of actual critical damage at level 1, scaling linearly to 60% at level 99, and simultaneously inflicts that same pre-mitigation amount as bonus Cold damage. The Cold echo uses Defense and Frost resistance, cannot critically strike, reflect, or recursively trigger Spirit Wounds, and overkill never increases the restored Mana. Penance restores mana after a successful buckler block equal to the prevented damage multiplied by Spirit and the same 1%–60% level-scaled fraction. Both cap restoration at maximum mana and can be locally toggled from the spell rail. Renewal Idols grant the extractable Rapid Regeneration self-buff: it costs 4 mana, has a 20-second base cooldown, casts automatically while missing health, and for 10–30 level-scaled seconds multiplies total health regeneration from 120% to 500% while adding 0.1 HP/second.
- The bottom-left spell area has a Learned Skills vertical pill and an adjacent Geared Skills vertical pill. The former contains every permanently learned skill; the latter contains only currently equipped skills not yet learned. Each slot includes its number-only effective level and independent cooldown state. Their cooldown is a circular clock mask, not a vertical fill. Every visible or temporarily previewed spell slot is pointer-hoverable and keyboard-focusable. Hovering or focusing one opens an unclipped tooltip to the right of the rails with its full name, gameplay description, resource, notable guaranteed status behavior, and a concise `Stat bonuses` sentence naming every stat that modifies that spell. Beneath the description, a two-column comparison shows `Current level` on the left and `Next level` on the right. Active-skill columns include capped effective level, cost, effective damage scaling when applicable, cooldown, and range or self-targeting. Passive-skill columns instead show capped effective level, continuous activation, effective resource upkeep, and only metrics used by that passive, such as conversion, pull speed, poison amplification, returned damage, movement or attack-speed multiplier, aura radius, pulse damage/interval, or cooldown removal; they never show zero-second cooldowns or irrelevant range. Blocking likewise shows its continuous Stamina upkeep in addition to the successful-block cost described by its contract. Each comparison column renders its properties as a single vertical label/value list so values remain unwrapped. Clicking a slot toggles it enabled or disabled without reordering. Disabled slots are dark gray and inactive; enabled slots retain their resource-colored border and can trigger. An enabled skill whose current Mana, Stamina, or Life does not satisfy its effective cast cost remains enabled but renders with a darker icon until it becomes affordable. One shared skill-property renderer accepts a level and is called once with the visible skill level and once with that level plus one. Spell borders communicate their resource: cyan-blue for mana, yellow for stamina, and red for life. A ready, enabled off-cooldown rail skill also emits a matching outer glow. Every enabled skill automatically casts immediately when its own cooldown, trigger condition, and full resource cost permit; one skill cooling down does not block another.
- Basic attacks and physical/block skills use stamina; magic and healing use mana. Enabled spell passives drain their contract's per-effective-level Mana upkeep continuously, while Blocking drains its per-effective-level Stamina upkeep continuously in addition to each successful block's cost. Mana-cost reduction applies to passive Mana upkeep. Upkeep is summed by resource for both heroes and spawned enemies; exhausting a resource suspends every upkeep skill using that resource until it regenerates to at least 1. Suspended skills have no effect. Mana and Stamina never fall below zero or exceed their maxima, HP never falls below zero, and all payments fail or clamp without overspending. Every blood spell prices its percentage against the caster's current remaining HP at cast time, after life-cost reduction, and clamps the HP actually spent so the caster retains at least 1 HP. A blood spell may cast whenever current HP is above 1; it cannot cast at exactly 1 HP. Rent and other ordinary blood attacks add the exact clamped HP actually spent to base attack damage before applying that spell's damage and level multipliers; Vampiric Boomerang uses its explicit `HP spent * (1 + skillLevel / 10)` damage rule. Rent spends 10% of remaining HP and uses a 1.25 skill multiplier. Rent is a universal learned full-circle physical attack with a 4-second cooldown and 180 base range. Its cast effect is a fast oval centered on the hero at the effective range, drawn as three tightly spaced 2-pixel red, white, and red weapon-edge lines. Spell targeting/effect range is `baseRange + min(300, 0.5 * skillLevel * Spirit)` pixels. Healing automatically casts below 75% health when its full mana cost is available. Each cast restores its 20%–90% level-scaled fraction of current HP plus 5% of maximum HP and another 5% of maximum HP multiplied by `currentStamina / maxStamina`, capped by missing HP, and spends 2 mana per HP actually restored. Its cooldown linearly scales from 15 seconds at level 1 to 1 second at level 99 and is not further modified by attributes, cooldown reduction, or weapon level. Bucklers grant the reactive Blocking spell. Every successful block costs at least 1 stamina and starts a 1-second cooldown; a Return buckler divides that cooldown by the equipped main hand's attacks per second. Percentage-based damage return adds a stat- and rarity-scaled stamina premium that upgrading reduces toward 1. Heroes and all spawned enemies share stats, resources, inventories, cooldowns, critical hits, healing, bleed, poison, and stun. Every creep renders a 2-pixel blue Mana bar directly below its HP bar and a 2-pixel yellow Stamina bar below Mana.
- Charms carry the extractable Vampiric Boomerang skill. It spends `max(3% of remaining HP, 1 HP)` after life-cost reduction, follows the shared 1 HP blood-spell floor, and adds `HP spent * (1 + skillLevel / 10)` to its base damage. It launches a red crescent in the current target direction. The crescent has a 33-pixel collision radius, travels outward and back at 90 pixels/second to its level-scaled maximum range, reverses, remains active through collisions, and deals its cast damage per second to every foe for the exact time each foe overlaps its broad collision area. When it reaches its caster, it totals the actual damage dealt across both legs and heals once for 1% of that cumulative damage at level 1, scaling linearly to 80% at level 99. Rent and other ready active spells use rotating ready-skill priority, so a very short equipped-skill cooldown cannot permanently starve Rent or another learned skill.
- Rare and Epic axes additionally carry extractable Whirlwind. It costs 3 stamina, has a 12-second base cooldown, and surrounds the moving hero with rapidly spinning physical weapon edges for 3 seconds at level 1, scaling linearly to 30 seconds at level 99. Its movement-speed multiplier likewise scales linearly from 0.5× at level 1 to 1.5× at level 99, pulses every 0.25 seconds for `1 + 0.4 * Strength` physical damage, and uses radius `90 + 1.2 * skillLevel` pixels. The persistent visual follows the hero and matches that radius and duration.
- Some generated weapons and offhand relics carry one sustain passive. Vampiric items heal their wielder for `damageActuallyDealt * (2% + 0.1% * Spirit)`. Vigorous items add `0.01 + 0.002 * Strength` HP/second. Sustain from equipped main- and offhand items stacks additively; life steal does not trigger from reflection, status damage, healing, or overkill beyond health actually removed.
- Relic perks are selected from a typed registry. Voodoo Doll grants passive Voodoo, multiplying poison damage applied by its wearer by `1 + min(1.5, 0.03 * Spirit)`. Ember Idol grants Fire Breath: a 1.1-damage forward cone with a 9-second base cooldown, 150 base range, 4 mana cost, and an eight-second Burn dealing `0.25 + 0.03 * Spirit` per tick. Renewal Idol grants Rapid Regeneration, a 4-mana automatic self-buff with a 20-second base cooldown that scales from 120% total health regeneration plus 0.1 HP/second for 10 seconds to 500% plus 0.1 HP/second for 30 seconds at level 99. Plain Spirit Relics have no additional perk.
- Weapons, affixes, skills, offhand perks, enemy archetypes, and their numeric definitions live in typed content registries. Serialized instances contain identifiers and data only; skill-specific execution is isolated behind handlers keyed by skill id. Registry metadata declares active/passive behavior, cost, range, cooldown, and enemy eligibility so hero and enemy casting share content configuration.
- One-handed generated enemy builds receive an offhand 25% of the time, split evenly between bucklers and relics. Bucklers are spiked 25% of the time. Spiked common/uncommon bucklers roll one reflection component, rare two, and epic all three. Half of relics carry Attraction; roughly one quarter of generated staves carry it.
- Waves 0 through 8 contain no enemy spellcasters or projectile/ranged combat builds. Their regulars and champions use only non-projectile melee weapons, and regulars receive no bonus skills. Ranged or spell-bearing queued equipment may ride on an early carrier as inactive carried loot but cannot replace its combat equipment; queued death echoes wait until wave 9. Beginning at wave 9, exactly `min(regularCount, 1 + round(N / 10))` regular creeps receive one enemy-eligible bonus skill from the registry. The assignment is server-authored and serialized in each unit build. Skilled creeps show a fire-colored outer ring and cast their bonus skill on its own cooldown; champions rely on their normal generated build and do not receive a bonus-skill assignment.

- Spell-rail tiles are click-only ordering buttons. They never locally suppress or equip skills: every available skill executes.
- Every spell tooltip displays its numeric cost together with the consumed resource instead of only naming the resource. Variable Healing cost is shown as 2 Mana per HP, reactive Blocking uses the equipped buckler's stamina cost, and passive skills show zero cost.

- Auras are extractable passive skills capped at level 99. Their shared radius is `180 + 120 * (level - 1) / 98 + min(300, 0.5 * level * Spirit)` pixels, so both effective skill level and Spirit enlarge the matching ground effect and combat area. Repeated extraction stacks permanent levels of the same aura, and different learned aura skills can run simultaneously. Glacial Aura uses a blue radial ground field and slows nearby enemy movement from 20% to 50%; Hindering Aura uses blue floor ripples and applies the same scaling to attack speed. Death Burst uses a green pointed aura and makes a nearby defeated foe deal 20% of its maximum HP as magic damage to other foes within 45% of the aura radius. Sunburn uses orange ground flares and damages all foes in range for `1% + 0.09% * Intelligence`, capped at 10% of each foe's maximum HP, every `max(0.5, 5 - 0.045 * Spirit)` seconds. Thunder Aura uses a translucent white electric field, pulses from 10 seconds at level 1 to 1 second at level 99, strikes one random foe for `3 + 0.6 * Intelligence` electric damage, and has `10% + normal critical chance`; a critical chains once for 60% damage.
- Each generated Scepter is a zero-hand offhand focus carrying one deterministic aura plus Spirit and Intelligence bonuses. Scepters have no basic attack, weapon damage, attack speed, stamina attack cost, projectile, or weapon affixes. They are excluded from ordinary enemy main-hand generation. Deterministic non-spiked Rare and Epic bucklers whose generation seed is divisible by five are Holy Bucklers carrying Blocking plus one deterministic aura. Aura passives consume no resource, and equipping or extracting these items uses the existing spell-level, range, and Gold previews.

## Waves and Rivals

- New heroes begin at wave 1. Wave N has `min(40, 10 + 2 * max(0, N - 1))` regular creeps, so the opening wave has 10 regulars. One regular template is rolled at `max(floor(heroLevel / regularCount), floor((N - 1) / 2))` and copied for the wave.
- Enemy levels 0 through 7 use fixed maximum health equal to `10 + level`, producing exactly 10 through 17 HP; wave 1 therefore starts with 10-HP creeps. Beginning at enemy level 8, maximum health is `5 * derivedMaxHP * enemyHealthMultiplier * 1.12^(floor(level) - 8)`, preserving the normal Strength and equipment contributions, applying the high-level enemy health baseline, and increasing it by 12% for every additional enemy level. Level 8 therefore uses five times the normal derived-stat and enemy-balance calculation without an exponential increase. A carrier with a player-sent item actually equipped has twice this final maximum health; an intro-restricted carrier that merely transports the sent item as inactive loot does not receive the multiplier.
- Regulars spawn in ten cumulative 10% batches, five seconds apart. Wave N normally adds `round(N / 15)` separately rolled golden champions after 75% of regulars, so the early waves contain no champions. On positive competitive wave numbers divisible by 10, one server-owned 50% roll may replace that normal champion set with realm-attacker clones.
- On every positive wave divisible by 10, the server independently has a 50% chance to query one persisted hero whose current level is in the inclusive range `N - 9` through `N`. When found, that hero's exact base stats and equipped main hand, offhand, amulet, and charm are copied into one golden boss creep at the normal preparation spawn time; backpack contents, currencies, and identity are not copied. The boss receives every skill that would be active for that hero, including equipment-granted and universally unlocked learned skills. Each serialized boss skill level uses the normal hero effective-level calculation, then is capped at the boss's level. The SQL lookup is limited to one randomly selected eligible row, and the boss remains a normal server-issued rival for reward and kill validation.
- A successful divisible-by-10 clone event creates one golden rival clone for every opponent attacking the current defender. Each clone copies that opponent's equipped main hand, offhand, and allocation tendencies, but not backpack contents. Its authored level is `floor(defenderLevel / opponentCount)`: one attacker receives the defender's level, two attackers each receive half, and three each receive one third, with integer truncation. Clone stats scale the copied allocation to that divided level, and rival XP and Gold use the same divided level. If the roll fails, or the wave is Solo/Training or has no opponent, normal champions use wave progression level `max(1, floor((N - 1) / 2) + 1)` and may copy the first opponent's allocation tendencies without copying equipment.
- Regular kills grant `10 + effectiveLevel` XP. Golden champion kills grant `25 + 3 * championLevel` XP. An active XP-send buff multiplies that earned reward after balance-profile and solo-wave modifiers. Sent equipment otherwise changes a carrier's build but never its XP value; the exact sent item instead participates first in the normal capped item-drop roll.
- Solo-wave kills grant 50% of the XP they would grant in competitive play, rounded down after the active balance-profile multiplier. Gold, drops, and score use their normal competitive rules.
- Every defeated enemy produces at most one server-owned walk-over reward: Gold, rarity Scrap, equipment, or nothing. The server first rolls Gold using the existing chance: regular enemies have 20% and rivals 50%, multiplied by the balance profile. A successful Gold roll creates a yellow circular pickup worth `1 + floor(level / 5)` for regulars or `3 + floor(level / 2)` for rivals and skips equipment rolls. If Gold fails, enemy equipment candidates independently roll their sparse item drop chances in order until one succeeds. A successful equipment roll has a 25% conversion chance to become Scrap instead: the Scrap uses that item's rarity, grants `max(1, ceil(itemLevel / 3))`, and renders as the same rarity-colored diamond silhouette as equipment but hollow. Otherwise the generated equipment appears as the existing filled rarity-colored diamond. Gold, Scrap, and equipment are credited only after collection; unknown or duplicate drop ids grant nothing.
- Hover highlights enemies. Clicking one replaces the hero identity/inventory HUD with its portrait, level, attributes, resources, statuses, weapon, and backpack. Empty-space click or Back restores the hero.

## Server Ownership

- The server owns progression persistence, allocation validation, XP/level changes, Gold/Scrap/equipment ground-drop rolls, rarity scraps, generation seeds, waves, realms, equipment, stacking, bulk-action loops, selling, purging, upgrading, sending, and inventory mutations.
- The client owns arena simulation and reports kills and collected generated drop IDs. Protocol shapes remain suitable for later validation and database persistence.
- The server retains a ledger of wave-issued units and generated ground drops. The client reports only unit ids and opaque drop ids; duplicate, unknown, or invalid-state reports grant nothing.
- Gold, Scrap, and equipment use the same orphan-reconciliation rules. A pickup is marked pending only after its WebSocket message is accepted for transmission. A connection loss or missing pickup acknowledgement must not leave it permanently pending: after reconnection or a bounded acknowledgement timeout, the client reports its active and pending drop ids for reconciliation. The server compares those ids with the player's current ground-drop ledger, instructs the client to remove client-only ids, reissues complete tagged drop payloads for server-only ids, and releases pending ids absent from the ledger. Reconciliation never grants currency or inventory directly and therefore cannot duplicate a collected reward; collection still requires the normal server-authoritative `collectDrop` command. A reissued server-only drop is placed at the hero so it remains recoverable despite its original client-owned position being unavailable.
- Reward probabilities and amounts are defined centrally by the single authoritative balance configuration shared by development and production.

## First-session UX

- Joining shows movement, automatic combat, and item-pickup guidance in the non-blocking HUD notice. Arena simulation and spawn timers begin without a confirmation modal.
- The character sheet is a fixed retractable left-side column and the permanent inventory is a separate fixed retractable right-side column. Gold, Souls, and rarity scraps appear in a non-scrolling header at the top of the right inventory column, immediately above its equipment count. Only the equipment-card list beneath that header scrolls, without a synthetic empty tile or extra card-sized padding at its end. The inventory panel itself and its resource header remain fixed. The expanded inventory is 640 pixels wide; each card places descriptive stats on the left and actions on the right. Inspection replaces the sheet only and leaves the player's inventory usable. The arena canvas and combat HUD occupy the space between the two columns as either column expands or collapses.
- Backpack item cards show only applicable attack damage, attack speed, weight, effects, skills, item level, and attribute requirements; absent attributes do not leave placeholder cells. Every item card shows an `Equip slot` row directly beneath `Requirements`, using `Main hand`, `Offhand`, `Amulet`, or `Charm` as appropriate; the row remains present when an item has no requirements. The local hero character sheet replaces full equipment cards with one fixed-height row of four compact, rarity-colored icons for main hand, offhand, amulet, and charm; each icon identifies its slot and item in a hover/focus tooltip. Inspected heroes and enemies retain their public equipment details. Left-clicking equipped main-hand, offhand, amulet, or charm equipment clears its respective slot. Equipped backpack cards do not repeat an `Equipped` text label; selection uses a light translucent fill matching the item's rarity edge plus tight inner and outer rarity glows that do not bleed materially beyond the card. Hovering any card adds the same rarity outline and tight shadows without applying the selection background. During an equip preview, every currently equipped inventory card that would be displaced receives a red inner/outer glow; previewing a two-handed weapon marks the current main hand and offhand when present but never either accessory, while previewing an amulet or charm marks only its matching accessory slot. Backpack actions are one full-width button per row in a single vertical column. Shift+click requests the applicable bulk action and button titles explain this shortcut. Hovering Sell previews the resulting Gold balance in green; hovering Purge previews the resulting matching-rarity Scrap balance in green; hovering Upgrade previews its resulting Gold and matching-rarity Scrap balances in red, including negative projected values when resources are insufficient. Inspected enemy equipment remains static.
- The inventory's fixed header shows a compact four-cell loadout row beneath currencies: Main hand, Offhand, Amulet, and Charm. Each cell always occupies the same space, shows the equipped item's rarity and name or `Empty`, and exposes the full slot/item label to assistive technology and a tooltip. Hovering or focusing an item card gives its destination loadout cell a conspicuous bright border, glow, and lift. Hovering or focusing an occupied loadout cell applies the same conspicuous emphasis to the exact equipped backpack card; an empty slot has no reverse card target. This makes an equipped copy in an `xN` backpack stack unambiguous: remaining enabled actions operate on spare copies and do not mean the item failed to equip.
- The bottom RPG HUD places a red gradient health bar on the left with a two-pixel yellow stamina strip beneath it, a circular XP ring around the current name/level badge in the middle, and a cyan-blue gradient mana bar on the right. Health/stamina and mana sit on subtle translucent, slightly blurred backdrops so they remain legible over combat. The health and mana headers retain current/maximum values and show their current effective regeneration as a `+x/s` value at the right end; health includes equipped Vigorous regeneration and mana includes the main-hand mana-regeneration multiplier. The XP ring shows within-level progress. The displayed XP eases by 10% of the remaining distance toward the latest authoritative XP target on each HUD frame; a newer reward retargets the animation from its current visual value without waiting for an earlier animation to finish. The visible ring and level are derived from this eased value. Positive hero statuses, including the current XP-send buff, appear above the Mana bar; harmful statuses appear above the Health bar. Allocation copy states that all five default points are assigned instead of presenting `0 remaining` without context.
- A wave has a three-second preparation delay. Off-screen enemies display edge indicators so the player understands where threats are approaching from.

- Generated weapon damage is `classDamage * (1 + 0.025 * level) * rarityPower`. A nonzero-level requirement is `max(1, floor(0.6 * level * rarityPower))`. Sell value is `max(1, round((level + 1) * rarityPower * (4 + 2 * affixCount)))`. Common/uncommon/rare/epic generation attempts 1/2/3/4 distinct compatible affixes; regular-wave templates request the reduced mode, which attempts one. Duplicate random affix picks are skipped rather than rerolled.
- Rusty adds `5% * rarityPower` poison to physical weapons; Venomous adds `10% * rarityPower` poison to sword, dagger, axe, or staff; Bleeding adds `8% * rarityPower` bleed to sword, dagger, or axe; Stunning adds `7% * rarityPower` stun to club, mace, or hammer; Focused adds `10% * rarityPower` magic amplification to staff; Swift adds `0.12 * rarityPower` to the attack-speed multiplier for every weapon class. Daggers additionally gain `4% * rarityPower` critical chance. Staves additionally gain `rarityPower` mana-regeneration multiplier and `12% * rarityPower` magic amplification.
- A generated weapon seed congruent to 1 modulo 7 grants the 2% Vampiric base; congruent to 2 grants Vigorous; no item receives both. A staff seed divisible by 4 grants Attraction. Upgrading preserves affixes, rarity, sustain passive, and class, increases level by one, and rerolls level-derived identifiers and values.
- Bucklers are spiked with 25% probability. Spiked Common/Uncommon roll one unique reflection component, Rare two, and Epic three from Flat, Strength, and Return. Every equipped buckler grants `5% * rarityPower` increased Gold pickup value and `2% * rarityPower` Rarity Boost. Rarity Boost is a server-owned chance for an equipment drop that would be collected at Common, Uncommon, or Rare to promote by one rarity while preserving its configuration and level within the new cap; Epic cannot promote. Their requirement is `max(1, floor(0.35 * level * rarityPower))` Strength, block base is `10% * rarityPower`, drop chance follows the standard formula, and sell value is `(level + 1) * rarityPower * 5` rounded for spiked or `* 4` otherwise. Relics use the analogous Spirit requirement, add `max(1, round(rarityPower))` Spirit, sell for the rounded `4 * (level + 1) * rarityPower`, roll Attraction 50% of the time, then independently use the next seeded roll for Vampiric below 25% or Vigorous below 50%.
- Skill spell power is `1 + 0.15 * (level - 1)`. Before other modifiers, base cooldown is divided by `max(1, Intelligence + Agility)`; Agility is the project's Dexterity attribute and the floor keeps zero-stat builds finite. Except for Flurry, cooldown duration then multiplies by `max(0.2, (1 - cooldownReduction) * (1 - min(0.5, 0.04 * (level - 1))))`. Flurry instead uses its own 10-to-1-second level scaling before the Intelligence/Agility and weapon-level modifiers, without this second level reduction. Automatic skills retain stable insertion order but rotate the starting position after each successful cast; the first ready and affordable skill from that position is used, preventing a short-cooldown weapon skill from starving Rent or other learned skills.
- Every non-passive skill in the rotating automatic-cast queue has a short authored base cast time appropriate to its effect. Effective cast time is `baseCastTime / (1 + 0.01 * (skillLevel - 1) + 0.0005 * Agility * skillLevel)`, with Agility serving as Dexterity, and is capped at `2 / attacksPerSecond`; it is never longer than two current basic-attack intervals. The chosen skill reserves the rotation position while winding up but pays no resource and starts no cooldown until completion. Losing its target, leaving its effective range, disabling it, entering cooldown, or becoming unable to pay cancels the wind-up and advances selection to the next eligible entry. A completed cast pays its resource, starts only that skill's cooldown, advances the stable queue, and immediately begins the next ready, affordable, in-range skill without waiting for weapon recovery; unused frame time carries into that next wind-up. Basic attacks run in parallel on their own attack cooldown and may fire during a skill wind-up, with a completing skill paying its resource before a same-frame basic attack. The active skill slot shows cast progress with an additional white inset and outer glow, and rotating active-skill current/next tooltip columns include the effective `Cast time`. Passive skills, reactive Blocking, and the separately triggered Healing and Rapid Regeneration skills remain instant and do not show cast time.

## Exact Wave Construction and Realm Queueing

- A regular wave uses one generated template copied for every regular slot, with unique unit ids. Each normal champion is generated separately and carries one additional reduced-affix weapon when its level is above zero; realm-attacker clones carry the copied equipped items and no backpack item. Regular kind is `bubbleShooter` exactly when its generated main hand is a staff, otherwise `melee`; champions and clones use the rival protocol archetype even when their weapon is a staff.
- Regular spawn index `i` uses batch `min(9, floor(i * 10 / count))` and time `3000 + batch * 5000` ms. Champions arrive at `3000 + floor(7.5 * 5000) = 40000` ms. Spawn records are sorted by time. Wave duration remains 60000 ms and the leave window opens after `3000 + 9 * 5000 = 48000` ms from the latest global dispatch.
- Random allocation creates five points per enemy level and scales opponent allocation weights to the rival level. XP is granted before deriving the persisted level; every crossed level adds the currently saved five-point allocation to attributes.
- Queued items replace a carrier's matching equipment and preserve the exact sent item's configuration and level. An equipped sent item raises the carrier to at least that item level. When this raises the carrier's level, all five attributes are regenerated from the carrier's deterministic allocation at the new level; each attribute named in the sent item's requirements is then clamped to at least the required value. The carrier's regular-enemy XP and Gold rewards use the resulting level. A sent weapon becomes main hand and clears offhand when two-handed. A sent offhand forces a two-handed generated carrier to reroll a one-handed physical weapon at the resulting carrier level, then occupies offhand. Intro-restricted items that are carried instead of equipped do not raise the carrier or its rewards. Carriers inherit sender id/name and backlash flag. Incoming sender queues plus backlash form round-robin sources; at most one queued item is assigned per regular slot and rivals never carry queued equipment.
- Drop candidates are checked in order: the exact sent item, generated main hand, generated offhand, then carried items, skipping duplicates of the sent slot. Each candidate makes its own capped drop roll and the first success becomes the sole ground drop. Training never reaches reward/drop resolution. Unknown or already-resolved unit ids are rejected, and an accepted issued unit is deleted before rewards so it can resolve only once.
- Spell tooltips are rendered outside overflow-clipping HUD containers and their measured positions are clamped to every viewport edge. A tooltip opens to the left when there is insufficient room on the right, and content taller than the available viewport scrolls inside it. Showing an overlay tooltip never makes the full-window game document scroll; only explicitly scrollable panels and an oversized tooltip provide their own local scrollbars.
- Amulets and charms may roll an integer 1–25 HP restored on each credited enemy kill or an integer 1–50 Mana restored on each credited enemy kill. These values stack across equipped accessories, are reduced by each item's requirement effectiveness, cap restoration at the corresponding resource maximum, retain their rolled values through upgrades, and appear in item details plus the Utility effective-stat sheet and equip previews.
