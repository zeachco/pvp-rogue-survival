# Progression, Items, and Rival Waves SPEC

This specification extends `specs/SPEC.md` and `specs/MECHANICS_SPEC.md` and is authoritative for progression and itemization.

## Progression

- Heroes begin at level 0 with zero Agility, Strength, Magic, Spirit, and Intelligence, 10 base maximum HP, 5 base maximum mana, and the specified Throwing Axe/Buckler starter loadout.
- Progression uses a quadratic cumulative curve: total XP required to reach level `L` is `15 * L²`. The incremental cost from level `L` to `L + 1` is therefore `15 * (2L + 1)`, producing `15, 45, 75, 105, 135, 165, ...`. Level lookup uses the inverse square-root formula with non-negative integer clamping. Persisted profiles retain their earned level by raising stored XP to at least `15 * savedLevel²` during migration. Each level grants five integer attribute points using editable non-negative weights totaling 5. Allocation uses one minus button, scalar value, and plus button per stat rather than raw number fields. Buttons clamp values to zero through the shared five-point budget; the panel shows used and remaining budget. Reset sets the pending allocation to all zero without saving. Save is enabled only at exactly five points, and the server independently validates non-negative integers totaling 5. Hovering or focusing the panel replaces each displayed current attribute with its projected next-level value and updates that preview as controls change; only values that actually increase are green, and no separate `+N` label is shown. A separate retroactive ratio reset submits the currently displayed valid five-point allocation, costs `100 * currentLevel` Gold, saves that allocation for future levels, and replaces every base stat with `allocationForStat * currentLevel`; insufficient Gold rejects the reset without mutation.
- Maximum HP and mana are derived rather than persisted: `maxHP = 10 + Strength` and `maxMana = 5 + 2 * Intelligence`. Existing heroes therefore receive current resource maxima automatically from their stored attributes after load. Strength also adds 0.2 base damage and 1 stamina per point. Agility contributes to one-handed handling and adds 1% critical chance. Magic remains an allocated attribute for spell-oriented progression without directly increasing the resource pool. Natural health regeneration is deliberately weak at `0.005 + 0.005 * Spirit` HP/second; Spirit continues to add 0.1/second mana and stamina regeneration. Intelligence also adds 5% critical damage, 1% cooldown reduction, and 2% magic amplification.
- Critical chance is capped at 75%, cooldown reduction at 60%, and base critical damage is 150%.
- Defeat outside Training resets cumulative XP, level, and earned base attributes to zero. Inventory, allocation, scraps, and learned skills persist. The defeated hero loses `floor(Gold / 2)` Gold and `floor(Souls / 2)` Souls; an attributable opposing-player killer receives those exact amounts, otherwise they are lost. Equipped items whose requirements are no longer met are retained in their stacks but unequipped, restoring the starter club when necessary.
- Voluntary defeat uses the same progression and currency reset and is allowed in Training Grounds. Every combat or voluntary defeat snapshots the pre-reset level, base stats, main hand, and offhand into one hostile rival echo queued for the highest-ranked durable hero when the defeated hero is not that recipient. The recipient is selected by level descending then username ascending and receives the echo in their next authored wave.
- XP, attributes, allocation, gold, souls, scraps, learned skills, learned skill levels, equipment, backpack stacks, and quantities survive defeat, username login, opaque-id reconnection, and server restarts through the Bun SQL hero blob.
- Souls are a persistent currency awarded one at a time for credited realm/player kills. The fixed header at the top of the right inventory column displays Gold, Souls, and the four rarity scrap balances in a six-cell grid that remains visible during enemy inspection and while the equipment list scrolls.
- Beneath the five allocated attributes, the character side panel places Next-level allocation immediately above Effective stats. It is collapsed by default to an unoutlined text label with no arrow indicator. Hovering it or focusing within it reveals its controls as a bordered panel and activates projected attribute/effective-stat differences; leaving collapses it again unless focus remains within. The effective-stat sheet is produced by one pure calculation from base attributes, main hand, and offhand, allowing the UI to evaluate current or mocked builds without mutating the hero. It includes weapon damage and attacks/second, attack resource cost, critical chance and multiplier, magic amplification, cooldown reduction, per-skill-level Spirit range gain, maximum health/stamina/mana, health/mana/stamina regeneration, Defense, block chance/cost, life steal, status chances, and Attraction. Hovering an inventory item recalculates it as an equip replacement: the current item in that slot is removed first, and a two-handed preview also removes the offhand. Changed effective values are green until preview ends. `Defense` is the current buckler mitigation cap (`Strength`) and is zero without a buckler; this label does not imply a separate always-on armor system. Zero values remain visible in this character summary so the full build can be compared at a glance.
- Hover-difference previews are presentation-only and never mutate authoritative progression. Allocation hover/focus and inventory-card equip previews project changed attributes and effective stats in green directly in the left character sheet. Action-button currency projections replace the affected Gold, Soul, or rarity-Scrap values in the fixed inventory header with `current → projected`, using green for gains and red for costs. Extract hover additionally projects each affected spell level in green directly in the bottom-left spell rail, including a temporary slot for a newly learned spell. Sell previews one copy's Gold gain, Purge previews one copy's matching-rarity Scrap gain, Upgrade previews one copy's Gold and matching-rarity Scrap costs plus the level+1 item result directly on the hovered inventory card, and Extract previews its Gold cost plus one permanent level for every extractable skill. Upgrade-card projections show level, attack values, effects, requirements, and every other changed item field as green `current → upgraded`. When the action upgrades the equipped copy, the same projected item also updates the equipped card and character stats in the left sheet. When an equipped stack has spare copies, Upgrade previews the spare-copy item result on the inventory card without changing the character sheet because the server upgrades spares first. Upgrade and Extract Gold projections may become negative to communicate shortages. Button hover previews always describe one action even though Shift+click may request a bulk loop. Leaving the button or card restores authoritative displayed balances, spells, equipment, and stats; no separate diff panel appears beneath inventory actions.
- Equip and Unequip hover use the same distributed preview: base/equipment-adjusted attributes and advanced combat stats update in the character sheet, while skills gained, lost, or level-changed update in the spell rail. Every previewable UI value is prepared as `{ currentVal, newVal }`, where `newVal` may be `null` for removal. Shared formatting renders unchanged values normally, changes as `current → new`, and removals as `current → —`; shared tone utilities apply green to improvements/gains and red to regressions/costs. Feature code supplies data rather than hand-formatting preview strings separately for resources, stats, advanced stats, or spells.

## Items and Skills

- Every generated item rolls defensive perks from Defense, Physical Resist, Magic Resist, Fire Resist, Frost Resist, Poison Resist, Bleed Resist, and Dodge. Common, Uncommon, Rare, and Epic items carry one, two, three, and four distinct parallel perks respectively. Item level supplies perk magnitude, with each perk rolling within its level-scaled range and reaching its declared maximum only at level 50; rarity increases breadth, not the maximum of one perk. Dodge is capped at 50%, each resistance at 50%, and Defense is flat damage reduction applied before percentage resistance. Physical and Bleed use Agility/Strength requirements, Magic and elemental resistance use Intelligence (Fire also Magic), Poison uses Spirit/Agility, Defense uses Strength, and Dodge uses Agility. Perk-derived requirements use five times item level distributed over their thematic attributes. Ordinary level upgrades retain perk types and reroll their magnitudes upward; rarity promotion adds newly random distinct perk types until the new rarity count is met.

- Hardcoded item definitions and randomized generators live in a shared configuration module. Generator inputs are level, rarity, class filters, and a deterministic seed; generated instances contain no functions.
- Weapon classes are club, sword, dagger, mace, axe, one-handed throwing axe, one-handed hammer, and staff. Newly created players equip a deterministic level-0 Common Throwing Axe and a deterministic level-0 Common Buckler, with one reserved copy of each in the backpack; this default does not rewrite existing saved equipment. Throwing axes make physical projectile basic attacks at a short 210-pixel range and have an inherent 15% chance to bleed on every direct hit. The plain starter club remains the zero-value fallback restored when a main hand is unequipped.
- Edged weapons may bleed; mace/club weapons may stun; venomous and rusty affixes may poison compatible weapons; staves may amplify magic and mana regeneration. Throwing axes may also roll the Bleeding, Rusty, Venomous, and Swift affixes.
- Requirements, damage, speed, affixes, skills, stat bonuses, modifiers, drop chance, and sell value scale with item level and rarity. Generated items use a deliberately sparse drop chance of `min(30%, 4% + 6% * rarityPower)`, currently about 10% for common through 16.6% for epic gear.
- Weapon attack timing is weight-based instead of using a global linear Agility multiplier. The handling stat is Agility for one-handed physical weapons, Strength for two-handed physical weapons, and the average of Strength and Spirit for magic/staff weapons. Base attack interval in seconds is `weight / (10 + 0.1 * handlingStat)`; displayed attacks per second is its reciprocal multiplied by item attack-speed modifiers such as Swift. Canonical weights are Dagger 8, Club 12, Sword 14, Staff 16, and Mace 18. This formula is shared by the HUD, hero combat, and generated enemies.
- Clubs, swords, daggers, maces, axes, throwing axes, and hammers require one hand; staves require two. A one-handed build may equip one buckler or relic offhand. Relics do not block. Some generated staves and relics carry Attraction, a passive that pulls all uncollected arena item drops toward the wearer at 35 pixels/second. Attraction does not stack between equipped items. Equipped items remain as reserved copies in their inventory stacks and count toward occupied capacity. Equipping another item does not consume its tile; any previously equipped item is also retained as an inventory copy. An equip is rejected if retaining equipment that predates the inventory would exceed capacity.
- Inventory capacity is `4 + ceil(playerLevel / 10)` occupied equipment stacks. Exact matching equipment shares quantity. Empty, unequipped stacks are removed. A new unmatched stored drop remains on the ground when all occupied slots are full.
- Inventory presentation never renders zero-quantity stacks, even if stale state arrives during migration. It groups equipped stacks first, then preserves acquisition order for all remaining stacks, with the latest acquisition at the bottom.
- Matching includes kind, class, level, rarity, hands, affixes, requirements, bonuses, modifiers, skills, stamina cost, and reflection components while ignoring instance id, seed, generated name, drop chance, and sell value.
- Selling, purging, extracting, sending, and upgrading normally affect one copy. Holding Shift while clicking any of those action buttons sends a bulk request; Sell, Purge, Extract, and Send consume every available copy in the clicked stack, multiplying their currency, scrap, skill-level, or queue result per copy, while Upgrade repeats until copies or resources run out. The server stops immediately when an iteration makes no progress. Equipped copies remain reserved from destructive actions. An equipped stack grays out and disables Sell, Purge, Send, and Extract for the entire stack, including any spare copies. Upgrade is disabled only when Gold or matching-rarity Scrap is insufficient: it upgrades spare copies first, then upgrades the equipped copy in place while preserving its equipped slot. Purging grants `max(1, ceil(itemLevel / 3))` scraps of the item's rarity.
- Upgrading consumes one copy with lower base costs that rise with direct item attributes. Let `attributePoints` be the sum of positive values in `item.statBonuses` and `attributeFactor = 1 + 0.1 * attributePoints`; Gold cost is `ceil(1.5 * sellValue * attributeFactor)` and same-rarity Scrap cost is `ceil(2 * (currentLevel + 1) * attributeFactor)`. It creates one level+1 configuration preserving class, rarity, affixes, and reflection components while recalculating level-derived values, so no duplicate equipment copy is required. When the consumed copy is equipped, its upgraded result immediately replaces it in the same equipment slot. The output requires a matching tile or free permanent tile. A Shift+click upgrade repeats upgrades from the clicked source stack while its current copies and resources permit; it does not create persistent automation or process future pickups.
- Equipment level caps are Common 10, Uncommon 15, Rare 30, and Epic 50. Generated equipment is clamped to its rarity cap. Upgrading a max-level Common, Uncommon, or Rare item promotes it to the next rarity and resets its level to 1 while preserving its equipment class, affixes, skills, sustain identity, attraction, and reflection configuration; the promoted rarity recalculates rarity-derived power, requirements, block values, drop value, and sell value. Epic level 50 is final and cannot be upgraded. Promotion uses the normal Upgrade action, costs based on the pre-promotion item, and participates in the same hover projection and Shift+click loop.
- Weapon level uses a primary multiplier of `1 + 0.025 * weaponLevel`. It scales weapon damage, stamina cost, item-granted critical/bleed/poison/stun chances, life-steal base, and the Strength coefficient of Vigorous regeneration. The equipped main hand also applies the smaller skill multiplier `1 + 0.005 * weaponLevel`: effective skill range is multiplied by it and effective skill cooldown is divided by it. These level multipliers stack with rarity, affixes, character stats, permanent skill levels, and cooldown reduction. Attack speed, weight, affix identity, and skill level are not directly increased by weapon level.
- Sending consumes one exact item and queues it for realm-wave use. If an equip swap cannot store old equipment, the old equipment becomes a server-confirmed ground drop.
- A creep equipped with a player-sent item has a visible outline matching that exact item's rarity color. The outline identifies player equipment regardless of whether the sent item replaced main hand or offhand; generated and merely carried equipment does not add this outline.
- All spells have a maximum effective level of 100. Skills carried by equipped main-hand or offhand equipment contribute one temporary skill level up to that cap. An unknown equipped skill is therefore available at level 1; if it is already permanently learned, equipping the item raises its effective level by one until unequipped without mutating permanent progression. Backpack equipment with at least one permanently extractable skill exposes an Extract action. Healing and the buckler-granted reactive Blocking skill are not extractable; an item carrying no other skill cannot be extracted and never consumes Gold. Extraction is server-owned, consumes that item, costs `10 * sellValue` gold, and permanently adds one level to each extractable skill up to level 100. Common through rare extraction still requires matching skill-bearing equipment to cast that learned skill. Extracting from Epic equipment additionally marks its skills as universal, allowing Arcane Bolt, Orbiting Hammers, and other extracted skills with any equipped weapon. Healing and Rent are universal defaults. Effective spell levels scale at least one meaningful parameter such as damage, healing, or cooldown.
- Every weapon, including common drops and the starter club, carries its class signature skill: clubs use Bash, swords Sweep, daggers Flurry, maces Shockwave, axes Cleave, throwing axes Rending Throw, hammers Orbiting Hammers, and staves Arcane Bolt. Cleave is a broad physical axe strike. Rending Throw is a short-ranged physical projectile that guarantees a standard bleed on hit. Orbiting Hammers costs 3 mana and automatically launches three magical hammer projectiles at evenly spaced angles; each spirals outward around the moving hero, damages the first enemy it intersects, and expires after 2.4 seconds. Shockwave is a full-circle physical impact with its own cooldown, damage multiplier, range, stun, and canvas effect; it is not an alias of Bash.
- Attraction relics also carry the extractable Force Field skill (retaining the stable `gravityPull` protocol/content id). It costs 8 mana and has an 18-second base cooldown. With a melee main hand it pulls every active enemy up to 180 pixels toward the hero; with a ranged or magic main hand it pushes every active enemy up to 180 pixels away. Rare and epic spiked bucklers carry two additional extractable skills: Thorns is passive and returns 5% of incoming direct damage even when an attack is not blocked; Reflective Surge costs 3 stamina, has a 16-second base cooldown, and for 6 seconds doubles Thorns and successful-block reflection while adding another 1% of incoming damage to every return. Rare and epic staves additionally carry Frozen Orb. Frozen Orb costs 10 mana, has a 20-second base cooldown, launches a slow moving orb that periodically emits damaging radial ice spikes, and applies Freeze from the orb and its spikes.
- The bottom-left spell bar shows learned spells and currently equipped main- and offhand skills, including their level and independent per-skill cooldown state. Every visible or temporarily previewed spell slot is pointer-hoverable and keyboard-focusable. Hovering or focusing one opens an unclipped tooltip to the right of the rail with its full name, gameplay description, effective level, resource, effective damage scaling when applicable, effective cooldown, effective range or self-targeting, and notable guaranteed status behavior. Its cooldown fill uses a bright striped overlay with a visible leading edge. Spell borders communicate their resource: cyan-blue for mana, yellow for stamina, and red for life. Skills automatically cast by stable priority when their own cooldown, resource, target, and health conditions permit; one weapon spell cooling down does not block another learned spell.
- Basic attacks and physical/block skills use stamina; magic and healing use mana; Rent costs 1 health and cannot cast at 1 HP. Rent is a universal learned physical attack with a 1.25 damage multiplier, 4-second cooldown, and 105 base range. Spell targeting/effect range is `baseRange + min(300, 0.5 * skillLevel * Spirit)` pixels. Healing automatically casts below 50% health when its full mana cost is available. Each cast restores a percentage of current HP, linearly scaling from 20% at level 1 to 90% at level 100, capped by missing HP, and spends 2 mana per HP actually restored. Its cooldown linearly scales from 60 seconds at level 1 to 1 second at level 100 and is not further modified by attributes, cooldown reduction, or weapon level. Bucklers grant the reactive Blocking spell. Every successful block costs at least 1 stamina and starts a 1-second cooldown; a Return buckler divides that cooldown by the equipped main hand's attacks per second. Percentage-based damage return adds a stat- and rarity-scaled stamina premium that upgrading reduces toward 1. Heroes and all spawned enemies share stats, resources, inventories, cooldowns, critical hits, healing, bleed, poison, and stun.
- Some generated weapons and offhand relics carry one sustain passive. Vampiric items heal their wielder for `damageActuallyDealt * (2% + 0.1% * Spirit)`. Vigorous items add `0.01 + 0.002 * Strength` HP/second. Sustain from equipped main- and offhand items stacks additively; life steal does not trigger from reflection, status damage, healing, or overkill beyond health actually removed.
- Relic perks are selected from a typed registry. Voodoo Doll grants passive Voodoo, multiplying poison damage applied by its wearer by `1 + min(1.5, 0.03 * Spirit)`. Ember Idol grants Fire Breath: a 1.1-damage forward cone with a 9-second base cooldown, 150 base range, 4 mana cost, and a four-second Burn dealing `0.25 + 0.03 * Spirit` per tick. Plain Spirit Relics have no additional perk.
- Weapons, affixes, skills, offhand perks, enemy archetypes, and their numeric definitions live in typed content registries. Serialized instances contain identifiers and data only; skill-specific execution is isolated behind handlers keyed by skill id. Registry metadata declares active/passive behavior, cost, range, cooldown, and enemy eligibility so hero and enemy casting share content configuration.
- One-handed generated enemy builds receive an offhand 25% of the time, split evenly between bucklers and relics. Bucklers are spiked 25% of the time. Spiked common/uncommon bucklers roll one reflection component, rare two, and epic all three. Half of relics carry Attraction; roughly one quarter of generated staves carry it.
- Each non-rival regular creep independently has a 5% chance to receive one enemy-eligible bonus skill from the registry. The assignment is server-authored and serialized in its unit build. Skilled creeps show a fire-colored outer ring and cast their bonus skill on its own cooldown; rivals rely on their normal generated build and do not use this rare roll.

## Waves and Rivals

- Wave N has `min(40, 10 + 2 * N)` regular creeps. One regular template is rolled at `max(floor(heroLevel / regularCount), floor((N - 1) / 2))` and copied for the wave.
- Regulars spawn in ten cumulative 10% batches, five seconds apart. A separately rolled rival spawns after 75% of regulars.
- A matched realm golden rival may copy an opponent's allocation tendencies, but its combat and item level is authored only by wave progression as `max(1, floor((N - 1) / 2) + 1)`. Unmatched and Training Grounds play generates the same level of independent rival. Neither player's permanent level changes rival difficulty or rewards.
- Regular kills grant `10 + effectiveLevel` XP. Golden rival kills grant `25 + 3 * rivalLevel` XP. Sent equipment changes a carrier's build but never its XP value; the exact sent item instead participates first in the normal capped item-drop roll.
- Solo-wave kills grant 50% of the XP they would grant in competitive play, rounded down after the active balance-profile multiplier. Gold, drops, and score use their normal competitive rules.
- Every defeated enemy produces at most one server-owned walk-over reward: Gold, rarity Scrap, equipment, or nothing. The server first rolls Gold using the existing chance: regular enemies have 20% and rivals 50%, multiplied by the balance profile. A successful Gold roll creates a yellow circular pickup worth `1 + floor(level / 5)` for regulars or `3 + floor(level / 2)` for rivals and skips equipment rolls. If Gold fails, enemy equipment candidates independently roll their sparse item drop chances in order until one succeeds. A successful equipment roll has a 25% conversion chance to become Scrap instead: the Scrap uses that item's rarity, grants `max(1, ceil(itemLevel / 3))`, and renders as the same rarity-colored diamond silhouette as equipment but hollow. Otherwise the generated equipment appears as the existing filled rarity-colored diamond. Gold, Scrap, and equipment are credited only after collection; unknown or duplicate drop ids grant nothing.
- Hover highlights enemies. Clicking one replaces the hero identity/inventory HUD with its portrait, level, attributes, resources, statuses, weapon, and backpack. Empty-space click or Back restores the hero.

## Server Ownership

- The server owns progression persistence, allocation validation, XP/level changes, Gold/Scrap/equipment ground-drop rolls, rarity scraps, generation seeds, waves, realms, equipment, stacking, bulk-action loops, selling, purging, upgrading, sending, and inventory mutations.
- The client owns arena simulation and reports kills and collected generated drop IDs. Protocol shapes remain suitable for later validation and database persistence.
- The server retains a ledger of wave-issued units and generated ground drops. The client reports only unit ids and opaque drop ids; duplicate, unknown, or invalid-state reports grant nothing.
- Gold, Scrap, and equipment use the same orphan-reconciliation rules. A pickup is marked pending only after its WebSocket message is accepted for transmission. A connection loss or missing pickup acknowledgement must not leave it permanently pending: after reconnection or a bounded acknowledgement timeout, the client reports its active and pending drop ids for reconciliation. The server compares those ids with the player's current ground-drop ledger, instructs the client to remove client-only ids, reissues complete tagged drop payloads for server-only ids, and releases pending ids absent from the ledger. Reconciliation never grants currency or inventory directly and therefore cannot duplicate a collected reward; collection still requires the normal server-authoritative `collectDrop` command. A reissued server-only drop is placed at the hero so it remains recoverable despite its original client-owned position being unavailable.
- Reward probabilities and amounts are defined centrally by the selected balance profile. Development and production currently use identical progression, reward, wave, and combat values; local development must not accelerate durable player advancement.

## First-session UX

- Joining shows movement, automatic combat, and item-pickup guidance in the non-blocking HUD notice. Arena simulation and spawn timers begin without a confirmation modal.
- As a temporary development-only debug convenience, the Vite development client automatically requests realm entry ten seconds after welcome if the player is still in Training Grounds. Production builds do not schedule this action, and any manual or server-confirmed realm transition cancels the pending request.
- The character sheet is a fixed retractable left-side column and the permanent inventory is a separate fixed retractable right-side column. Gold, Souls, and rarity scraps appear in a non-scrolling header at the top of the right inventory column, immediately above its equipment count. Only the equipment-card list beneath that header scrolls; its end includes empty padding equal to one full equipment-card slot so the final card can scroll clear of the bottom edge. The inventory panel itself and its resource header remain fixed. The expanded inventory is 640 pixels wide; each card places descriptive stats on the left and actions on the right. Inspection replaces the sheet only and leaves the player's inventory usable. The arena canvas and combat HUD occupy the space between the two columns as either column expands or collapses.
- Equipped-item cards in the character sheet and every backpack item card show only applicable attack damage, attack speed, weight, effects, skills, item level, and attribute requirements; absent attributes do not leave placeholder cells. An equipped backpack tile offers Unequip: offhands clear their slot, while unequipping a main hand restores the retained starter club. Backpack actions are one full-width button per row in a single vertical column. Shift+click requests the applicable bulk action and button titles explain this shortcut. Hovering Sell previews the resulting Gold balance in green; hovering Purge previews the resulting matching-rarity Scrap balance in green; hovering Upgrade previews its resulting Gold and matching-rarity Scrap balances in red, including negative projected values when resources are insufficient. The hero's active main-hand card uses a subtle gray background sweep tied to actual basic-attack recovery progress; inspected enemy equipment remains static.
- The bottom RPG HUD places a red gradient health bar on the left with a two-pixel yellow stamina strip beneath it, a circular XP ring around the current name/level badge in the middle, and a cyan-blue gradient mana bar on the right. Health/stamina and mana sit on subtle translucent, slightly blurred backdrops so they remain legible over combat. The health and mana headers retain current/maximum values and show their current effective regeneration as a `+x/s` value at the right end; health includes equipped Vigorous regeneration and mana includes the main-hand mana-regeneration multiplier. The XP ring shows within-level progress. The displayed XP eases by 10% of the remaining distance toward the latest authoritative XP target on each HUD frame; a newer reward retargets the animation from its current visual value without waiting for an earlier animation to finish. The visible ring and level are derived from this eased value. Allocation copy states that all five default points are assigned instead of presenting `0 remaining` without context.
- A wave has a three-second preparation delay. Off-screen enemies display edge indicators so the player understands where threats are approaching from.

- Generated weapon damage is `classDamage * (1 + 0.025 * level) * rarityPower`. A nonzero-level requirement is `max(1, floor(0.6 * level * rarityPower))`. Sell value is `max(1, round((level + 1) * rarityPower * (4 + 2 * affixCount)))`. Common/uncommon/rare/epic generation attempts 1/2/3/4 distinct compatible affixes; regular-wave templates request the reduced mode, which attempts one. Duplicate random affix picks are skipped rather than rerolled.
- Rusty adds `5% * rarityPower` poison to physical weapons; Venomous adds `10% * rarityPower` poison to sword, dagger, axe, or staff; Bleeding adds `8% * rarityPower` bleed to sword, dagger, or axe; Stunning adds `7% * rarityPower` stun to club, mace, or hammer; Focused adds `10% * rarityPower` magic amplification to staff; Swift adds `0.12 * rarityPower` to the attack-speed multiplier for every weapon class. Daggers additionally gain `4% * rarityPower` critical chance. Staves additionally gain `rarityPower` mana-regeneration multiplier and `12% * rarityPower` magic amplification.
- A generated weapon seed congruent to 1 modulo 7 grants the 2% Vampiric base; congruent to 2 grants Vigorous; no item receives both. A staff seed divisible by 4 grants Attraction. Upgrading preserves affixes, rarity, sustain passive, and class, increases level by one, and rerolls level-derived identifiers and values.
- Bucklers are spiked with 25% probability. Spiked Common/Uncommon roll one unique reflection component, Rare two, and Epic three from Flat, Strength, and Return. Every equipped buckler grants `5% * rarityPower` increased Gold pickup value and `2% * rarityPower` Rarity Boost. Rarity Boost is a server-owned chance for an equipment drop that would be collected at Common, Uncommon, or Rare to promote by one rarity while preserving its configuration and level within the new cap; Epic cannot promote. Their requirement is `max(1, floor(0.35 * level * rarityPower))` Strength, block base is `10% * rarityPower`, drop chance follows the standard formula, and sell value is `(level + 1) * rarityPower * 5` rounded for spiked or `* 4` otherwise. Relics use the analogous Spirit requirement, add `max(1, round(rarityPower))` Spirit, sell for the rounded `4 * (level + 1) * rarityPower`, roll Attraction 50% of the time, then independently use the next seeded roll for Vampiric below 25% or Vigorous below 50%.
- Skill spell power is `1 + 0.15 * (level - 1)`. Before other modifiers, base cooldown is divided by `max(1, Intelligence + Agility)`; Agility is the project's Dexterity attribute and the floor keeps zero-stat builds finite. Cooldown duration then multiplies by `max(0.25, (1 - cooldownReduction) * (1 - min(0.5, 0.04 * (level - 1))))`. Automatic skill priority is stable insertion order: equipped weapon skills first, then learned skills not already present. The first ready skill is considered; insufficient resources for it falls back to a basic attack rather than checking a later skill.

## Exact Wave Construction and Realm Queueing

- A regular wave uses one generated template copied for every regular slot, with unique unit ids. The rival is generated separately and carries one additional reduced-affix weapon when its level is above zero. Regular kind is `bubbleShooter` exactly when its generated main hand is a staff, otherwise `melee`; rivals use the rival archetype even when their weapon is a staff.
- Regular spawn index `i` uses batch `min(9, floor(i * 10 / count))` and time `3000 + batch * 5000` ms. The rival arrives at `3000 + floor(7.5 * 5000) = 40000` ms. Spawn records are sorted by time. Wave duration remains 60000 ms and the leave window opens after `3000 + 9 * 5000 = 48000` ms from the latest global dispatch.
- Random allocation creates five points per enemy level and scales opponent allocation weights to the rival level. XP is granted before deriving the persisted level; every crossed level adds the currently saved five-point allocation to attributes.
- Queued items replace a carrier's matching equipment. A sent weapon becomes main hand and clears offhand when two-handed. A sent offhand forces a two-handed generated carrier to reroll a one-handed physical weapon, then occupies offhand. Carriers inherit sender id/name and backlash flag. Incoming sender queues plus backlash form round-robin sources; at most one queued item is assigned per regular slot and rivals never carry queued equipment.
- Drop candidates are checked in order: the exact sent item, generated main hand, generated offhand, then carried items, skipping duplicates of the sent slot. Each candidate makes its own capped drop roll and the first success becomes the sole ground drop. Training never reaches reward/drop resolution. Unknown or already-resolved unit ids are rejected, and an accepted issued unit is deleted before rewards so it can resolve only once.
