# Mechanics SPEC

This specification extends `specs/SPEC.md` and is authoritative for arena simulation, movement, targeting, telegraphs, projectiles, collision resolution, and local defeat reset behavior. Progression, itemization, stat formulas, wave composition, generated builds, XP, drops, and rival scaling belong in `specs/PROGRESSION_SPEC.md`. Individual spell contracts live in `specs/spells/`; item-applied slow contracts live in `specs/item-effects/`.

The HUD groups every timed hero effect above the Health bar: harmful statuses are red-outlined, while positive buffs are green-outlined. Positive timed buffs include XP Send, Reflective Surge, and Rapid Regeneration. This rule supersedes older placement descriptions elsewhere in this file.

Spell presentation uses ownership color as its highest-priority readability cue. Enemy-authored spell effects, projectiles, cast flashes, telegraphs, and spell attack areas can never be greenish and are always tinted hostile red regardless of their ordinary elemental or resource color. Hero-authored spell effects, projectiles, cast flashes, telegraphs, and spell attack areas can never be reddish; formerly blood-red hero spells use a purple family, including Rent, Rending Throw, and Vampiric Boomerang. Red arena spell presentation therefore always communicates enemy ownership, while green presentation can never be mistaken for an enemy heal.

Superseding the older hero-light height description below, the hero's warm-white spotlight sits at `z = 72`, safely above its complete 50-unit silhouette, and uses a 62-degree half-angle. Its roughly 135-unit floor radius is wider than the former pool, while the lower source produces longer, more readable shadows from nearby obstacles without placing the light inside the hero model.

## Unarmed Combat

An empty main-hand slot resolves to an unarmed physical attack profile: `1 + effective Strength` base damage, 1.4 m range, and one attack per second. Basic weapon and unarmed attacks cost no Rage; each basic attack the local hero launches grants 3 Rage at the moment of the swing, whether or not it lands, and no longer restores an authored base Rage value on a damaging hit. Punches use the ordinary critical-hit and target-mitigation rules, but have no weapon affixes or weapon/offensive active skills. Independent offhand and globally bound effects—including Healing, auras, Attraction, and buckler Blocking—remain available. Empty-handed clones and death echoes use this same profile.

Every direct weapon or spell hit declares either a linear or radial force, or explicitly declares no force. A force applies only when the hit actually removes HP: dodged, fully blocked, fully mitigated, periodic, reflected, and self-paid damage never displace a unit. The baseline impulse is derived from the attacker's effective Strength and movement speed at emission. A linear force follows the attack or projectile's emitted travel vector; a radial force follows the normalized vector from its emission point to the hit unit. The hit stores this emission data, so a moving caster cannot turn a launched projectile's force after release. Skill execution metadata may scale or replace the baseline for stronger impacts.

Rent restores 1% of the caster's maximum HP for each distinct enemy touched by its attack area.

All blood skills (skills configured to spend Life) require the caster to have at least 30% of maximum HP before casting. This is an availability threshold checked before the skill's Life cost is paid; reaching exactly 30% permits the cast.

The rendered arena floor begins at world `z = -0.1`, superseding the earlier experimental `-0.0001` separation; its highest decorative layer remains below the `z = 0` gameplay/effect plane. Vertical left- or right-button dragging uses inverted local-X camera rotation: dragging down raises the camera pitch and dragging up lowers it, within the authored 8-to-85-degree limits.

## Simulation Loop

Floating combat numbers spawn at the visual center of the affected unit and rise vertically from there. Ordinary damage, healing, dodge, and block text renders at 60% of the authored combat-text size; critical damage retains the full 100% size.

Every camera-facing airborne projectile, including Arcane Bolt and fallback projectile art, is bottom-aligned above the gameplay plane using its complete visible bounds plus a 2-logical-pixel clearance. Gold, Scrap, and equipment pickups use the same clearance and include their outer glow in those bounds, so no visible portion intersects the rendered floor at any camera pitch.

The loaded 3D hero uses the normal full 50-logical-pixel presentation footprint. Player-sent invaders reuse that exact full-size hero model. Rival clones and death echoes reuse the hero model at 75% scale, for a 37.5-logical-pixel footprint. Their authored collision radii remain unchanged. Hero, invader, and clone presentation never creates or retains image-sprite planes; lightweight procedural geometry is used only while the model loads or after a load failure.

Each creep's complete Health, Mana, and Rage bar stack billboards slightly in front of that creep along the camera-facing normal and ignores scene depth occlusion, preventing sprite silhouettes from intersecting or covering the bars.

The chase camera starts at its maximum allowed distance from the hero. Wheel zoom may move it closer and back out, but never farther than this initial view.

Every active creep outside the perspective camera frustum shows a camera-facing directional arrow inset from the nearest viewport edge. Arrow visibility and direction derive from camera projection rather than comparing arena coordinates with browser pixels, so they remain correct across camera yaw, pitch, zoom, and viewport size.

Attacking, winding up an attack, and casting do not reduce movement speed. When a unit actually loses HP from received damage, its movement speed is reduced to 48% for 0.35 seconds; repeated damaging hits refresh that duration. Dodged, blocked, fully mitigated, and self-paid spell costs do not trigger this slowdown.

- The client owns local arena simulation for the active combat slice.
- A fixed client update advances hero input, acceleration-based movement, creep steering, attack telegraphs, hit areas, projectiles, collisions, local wave spawn timing, and camera following.
- Rendering draws a static, restrained Tron-style arena beneath the hero and combat presentation. Its default environment is deliberately dark, using a near-black blue clear color and subdued ambient and global directional lighting so local character and spell lights remain legible. Its dark board has a physically lit, uniform dark surface with fine cyan grid lines, brighter major lanes, and a cyan perimeter; it has no baked radial texture or center glow, scanning bands, moving lane lights, pulsing perimeter lights, or animated grid opacity. Real scene lights are the only localized illumination on the floor. The floor receives dynamic shadows but never casts one. Gameplay ground is world `z = 0`, while the rendered floor stack begins at `z = -0.1`; every decorative floor layer remains below zero so selections and ground effects cannot be coplanar with or clipped by it. Simulation remains two-dimensional and unchanged. World-standing 3D hero, regular-creep, golden-champion, clone/death-echo, and boss models are bottom-aligned to the gameplay plane. Every remaining camera-facing unit, resource, and equipment sprite places its visual center at half its authored presentation height above that plane so its lower edge meets the ground instead of clipping through it. Ground effects, selection rings, attack telegraphs, and range polygons remain at `z = 0` with only their own minimal ordering offsets. The local hero uses a pearl-and-teal world-standing animated 3D construct with a 50-logical-pixel presentation footprint while retaining its authored 18-logical-pixel collision radius, facing indicator, status overlays, and equipment-driven combat presentation. It carries one strong warm-white downward spotlight well above its complete silhouette; the broad cone runs from top to bottom across the hero, visibly illuminates the hero model itself, and forms a clearly readable contained pool substantially wider than the hero on the physical arena floor instead of radiating equally from the hero's center. Persisted-player bosses use a larger dark-and-gold world-standing animated 3D construct and carry one short-radius red point light. Regular creeps use a small red world-standing animated 3D construct without a character light, golden champions use a larger gold world-standing animated 3D construct with one short-radius yellow light, and rival clones/death echoes reuse the hero construct at a 37.5-logical-pixel footprint with one short-radius purple light. Invaders have no character light. Hero, creep, champion, clone/death-echo, and boss models rotate around world Z toward their authored facing and play idle, movement, attack, hit, and defeat states when supplied by the asset. Unit image sprites are not used. Lightweight procedural bodies provide loading and failure fallback presentation; modeled units detach that fallback once their 3D model loads. Every localized character and spell light is positioned above the `z = 0` gameplay plane, belongs to its complete source object, follows that source's simulation position when applicable, and disappears with it, allowing its falloff to illuminate the physical floor and nearby geometry within its authored shape. The graphics lighting mode controls their visibility, including lights created later: Off keeps only stronger ambient illumination, Hero only keeps the hero light plus mild ambient illumination, and All enables every global and local authored light while reducing ambient illumination to about 5%. The complete non-3D creep presentation, including its outline and authored facial details, billboards as one camera-facing plane; loaded 3D models remain world-oriented. Spell projectiles use vibrant unlit materials so their authored colors do not react to scene lights. Gold and Scrap pickup presentations likewise use unlit colors, neither cast nor receive shadows, and billboard as complete camera-facing groups while their simulation positions remain on the arena plane. Equipment pickups retain their authored lighting and shadow behavior. Every enemy retains its authored collision radius, selection ring, resource bars, sender label, and combat telegraphs; only its presentation role selects the model. Boss defeat removes the unit from simulation and reports rewards immediately while a render-only defeat model may remain for at most 1.2 seconds. The hero defeat model remains visible through the existing 1.8-second local-reset presentation. Each enemy's stacked resource display is anchored at least eight logical pixels beyond the top of its complete authored presentation silhouette and renders in front of every unit body even when the local hero or another unit overlaps it: Health is red, Mana remains blue, and Rage remains gold; an optional sender label sits above that stack without overlap. Active statuses visibly affect their unit: Freeze has highest tint priority and applies a light-blue tint; then Burn applies a fire tint; Poison applies a light-green tint; and Curse applies a dark-purple tint. Bleed does not tint, instead emitting tiny blood particles. Stun and Shock draw a small rotating white asterisk above the unit. The hero HUD renders harmful status icons above its Health bar and positive status icons above its Mana bar. Duplicate instances of the same displayed status are aggregated into stacks. Hovering or keyboard-focusing an icon opens a tooltip with its name, longest remaining duration, stack count when applicable, and combined damage per second for damaging statuses. A creep whose health was actually reduced flashes brighter for exactly its next rendered frame; this is shared hit feedback and applies to direct, status, aura, reflection, and Thorns damage without creating a projectile or spell-effect animation.
- The camera is a perspective third-person RPG chase camera that follows the hero from a behind-and-above, slightly over-the-shoulder view and looks ahead so the hero remains below the center aiming point. Its default chase distance is the former maximum zoom-out distance, and its new maximum zoom-out distance is exactly 1.5 times that former maximum. Holding either mouse button locks and recenters the pointer on the arena canvas; relative horizontal mouse delta orbits the camera around world Z, while relative vertical delta raises or lowers its pitch without allowing the cursor to reach a screen edge. Releasing the held button exits pointer lock, restores the cursor, and preserves both camera angles. Pointer capture remains the fallback when pointer lock is unavailable. Left-button camera movement never changes the hero's facing direction. Pressing the right button immediately selects the closest living enemy and turns the camera and hero toward it; a non-blocking reticle appears at the exact viewport center for the duration of the hold. Subsequent mouse motion freely adjusts the camera aim and hero facing. Pitch is clamped from 8 degrees above the gameplay plane to 85 degrees for a nearly top-down view, so the chase camera can neither pass below the ground nor overturn past its top view. WASD movement is camera-relative, so W moves toward the camera's forward ground direction and A/D strafe regardless of orbit yaw. An ordinary wheel gesture changes chase distance. Holding Shift while using the wheel changes camera pitch within the same bounds. Airborne projectile sprites, complete pickup presentations, procedural unit fallbacks, and enemy resource stacks billboard toward it; world-standing hero, creep, champion, clone/death-echo, and boss models and airborne 3D projectile models retain world orientation; and arena geometry, selection rings, auras, Healing, swamps, cast flashes, attack/range polygons, and other ground effects remain grounded. Hover and click targeting raycast onto the arena plane so they remain aligned in perspective.
- Gold, Scrap, and equipment presentations are centered above the arena plane without changing their logical positions, collision radii, values, or pickup overlap. Gold never uses a money-bag presentation. Each Gold pickup greedily decomposes its exact value into distinct thick, double-faced cylinder coin objects: a brown coin represents 1 Gold, a silver coin replaces 5 brown coins and represents 5 Gold, a gold-colored coin replaces 5 silver coins and represents 25 Gold, and a blue-diamond-colored coin represents 625 Gold. For example, 24 Gold renders as four silver and four brown coin objects, while 25 and 625 Gold each render as one coin object of their respective color. All coins begin at the pickup's presentation origin, receive deterministic visual-only displacement velocities at five times the original scatter so the cluster separates naturally, and scatter only along presentation-local X and upward Z while presentation-local Y remains zero so billboard rotation cannot place them beneath the arena. They idle with a subtle 2-logical-pixel vertical bob and continuously spin around their presentation-local Y axes. Scrap renders as a hollow rarity-colored lozenge whose complete silhouette uses the same bob and local-Y spin. Equipment does not inherit the resource animation.
- Every Gold coin and Scrap lozenge uses the same neutral matcap texture, tinted to its denomination or rarity color, so resource bodies show a view-dependent metallic shine without consuming scene lights or environment reflections. Coin spin and Scrap local-Y rotation move that baked highlight across their surfaces. Scrap retains its strong hollow outline with only a faint translucent metallic interior. Resource glows remain unlit flat-color accents, and no resource adds a light source.
- Every uncollected Gold, Scrap, and equipment pickup has a shared logical planar velocity. Attraction accelerates each pickup toward the attracting hero, with resulting Attraction movement capped at the authored 0.7 m/s rate. Shockwave and Force Field momentarily add a smaller outward impulse to pickups inside their effect, capped at 1.8 m/s total pickup speed and damped quickly. Pickup movement remains arena-bounded, so repeated spell impulses cannot eject or permanently strand drops away from the hero.
- Arena edges do not move or expand. The camera may show empty space beyond the arena when the hero is near an edge at moderate zoom, so the hero remains centered.
- Simulation state is advanced by focused systems over an explicit arena state. Systems receive time and random sources as inputs and emit typed events for networking and presentation; they never directly access the DOM or WebSocket transport.
- With the same initial state, fixed-step inputs, balance profile, and random seed, combat calculations and emitted arena events are deterministic.

## Arena and Movement

- When lighting is Off, every mesh uses an unlit material and keeps its authored color instead of being darkened by absent lights.

- The hero and creeps use velocity-based movement rather than waypoint movement.
- A desired movement direction is converted into velocity using acceleration and capped by maximum speed for self-propelled movement. External impulses may temporarily exceed that movement speed.
- Direction changes occur over successive fixed updates, producing turn/steering response instead of instantaneous full-speed changes. Creep velocity approaches its desired movement velocity over time; the same response acts as friction when steering stops and gradually dissipates external impulses.
- The hero and every active creep are solid circles at their authored collision radii. Active creeps also collide with one another and cannot stack. Unit overlap is resolved deterministically in stable hero-then-creep order with equal displacement assigned to both units. Closing normal velocity is shared equally while tangential velocity is preserved, so contacting units slide around one another and a crowd can physically shove the hero. Resolution uses only hard contact at the combined radii: creeps do not add predictive avoidance or extra personal-space buffers. Bounded repeated contact passes reconcile unit contacts with columns and arena bounds, including contacts produced by ordinary steering, attack wind-up, stun or Freeze sliding, and external force.
- Units decelerate when they have no desired movement. Ordinary impacts add their force to current velocity. Force Field never teleports a target: inside its level-scaled 4–16 m radius it deals up to `0.6 * spellPower(level)` magic damage and replaces each damaged target's current velocity with up to a 3.6 m/s radial launch away from its caster, regardless of the equipped main hand. Damage and launch are each multiplied by `max(0, 1 - distance / radius)`, so targets at the radius are unaffected. Existing inward momentum therefore cannot weaken or reverse a nonzero push. Applying the launch interrupts a creep target's current attack: internal ranged wind-up is cleared and an unresolved melee/area telegraph from that creep is invalidated. The same cast cancels every hostile projectile whose center is inside its current radius; friendly projectiles continue normally. On every cast, the caster randomly selects one active status instance, removes that exact stack from itself, and applies an equivalent fresh instance to every target actually damaged by the Force Field; a caster without statuses transfers nothing. The selected status is sampled per stack, so duplicate stacks are proportionally more likely. Force Field gives nearby uncollected equipment, Gold, and Scrap only the capped, rapidly damped pickup impulse defined above.
- Performing an attack temporarily slows the attacker. This applies to both the hero and attacking creeps.
- Heroes, creeps, and item drops outside the arena receive a 0.6 m/s correction toward the nearest fully legal position. Once fully inside they cannot cross out again; outward velocity is removed while tangential motion and lifetime continue. Projectiles are not corrected, clamped, or destroyed by arena bounds and remain active until their normal lifetime, hit, return, cancellation, or obstacle collision ends them. Fixed attack telegraphs do not move.
- Each client arena session generates ten random solid obstacles and preserves that layout across waves. Every obstacle independently renders as a cube, cylinder, or cone while retaining the same circular collision footprint. Obstacle bodies use physically lit standard materials, so the darker global environment and nearby character and spell point lights visibly affect them instead of rendering them at a fixed unlit color. Obstacles stay at least 3.6 m from the hero's starting point and 2 m from arena edges. They block hero and creep movement with sliding circle collision but do not block drops. Every hero or creep projectile, including persistent, emitted, orbiting, and returning projectiles, is destroyed immediately when its collision radius touches an obstacle.
- Creeps initially spawn just outside a randomly selected arena edge and enter immediately. Off-map death drops move inward until collectible.
- When the hero has operational Attraction, every active uncollected item drop moves directly toward the hero before arena-boundary correction and overlap collection. Attraction speed scales from the source item's requirement-adjusted base speed at level 1 to four times that speed at level 99. The passive changes only client presentation and pickup proximity; the server remains authoritative over whether the opaque drop id can be collected.
- With no obstacles in the current slice, each melee creep steers directly toward the hero only while outside its attack range and stops self-propelled movement at or inside that range, including while its attack is cooling down. Ranged creeps approach only while outside their attack range, stop through the remainder of that range, and retain their authored retreat behavior below their minimum distance. Hard unit contact preserves tangential motion so blocked creeps slide around one another while continuing to pursue; there is no separate neighbor-avoidance steering.

## Hero Control and Targeting

- Equipping or assigning an active spell enables its persisted auto-fire state by default, so its rail badge immediately shows the four gold selection corners. The player may still right-click the equipped badge to turn auto-fire off or back on. Reassigning a spell enables auto-fire for the assigned spell and removes the displaced spell from auto-fire. Existing persisted loadouts retain their saved auto-fire choices until changed. Passives remain always active through availability and never enter the auto-fire list.

- The hero is controlled with WASD, including equivalent lowercase and uppercase key events.
- While the right mouse button is held on the arena canvas, the hero immediately faces the camera's horizontal forward direction. The closest living enemy is acquired instantly on press and visibly selected; this initial lock does not remain sticky when the player moves the camera. The aiming override follows camera yaw continuously and takes priority over movement-facing and automatic closest-creep facing until the button is released, so every directional attack and spell resolves through the center reticle. For the duration of the hold, a teal line lies on the arena plane from the hero along that live facing direction to five times the equipped main-hand weapon's maximum range. It fades continuously from opaque at the hero's feet to transparent at its far endpoint. A teal circular indicator continues to show the true weapon range at 25% opacity. Both ground guides are hidden when no main-hand weapon is equipped and disappear immediately when aiming ends.
- Unless right-button aiming is active, the hero continuously turns along the shortest angular path toward the closest living creep whose world position is inside the active perspective-camera viewport. This auto-facing is independent of movement, cooldowns, resources, and attack range: camera-relative WASD movement never redirects hero facing, including while moving or strafing. The maximum turn speed is `300 + 57 * min(Agility, 100)` degrees per second: zero Agility preserves the existing 300-degree/second rate, while 100 Agility caps it at a near-instant 6,000 degrees per second. If no living creep is in view, the hero retains its current facing.
- Every directional hero weapon attack and spell emits along the hero's current facing at resolution time. Closest-creep selection still controls automatic eligibility and range, but never silently redirects an attack, projectile, boomerang, or ground-targeted cast toward that creep. Self-centered and radial skills remain centered on the hero.
- The equipped weapon attacks the closest creep automatically when cooldown, resource, target, range, and health conditions allow.
- The editable spell loadout contains six ordered slots for non-passive spells. Only equipped spells can be cast; slot order assigns keyboard shortcuts 1 through 6. Pressing a shortcut during play requests that spell once. Pressing it while the Available Spells modal is open instead assigns the hovered or keyboard-focused acquired spell to that exact slot, replacing its prior occupant and removing any duplicate occurrence. Right-clicking an equipped spell toggles its persisted auto-fire state; auto-fire spells use the rotating automatic cast queue. A rotating automatic skill first completes its shared progression-defined cast time. Its gameplay effect, resource payment, cooldown, and rotation advance occur together at completion, then the next eligible auto-fire skill begins immediately without waiting for basic-attack recovery. Basic attacks use an independent timer and may resolve during skill wind-up; a completing skill pays first when both resolve in one simulation update. While winding up, its HUD slot gains a white inset/outer progress glow. Invalidating its target, range, equipped state, auto-fire state for an automatic cast, cooldown readiness, or affordability cancels the pending cast without payment. Passive skills are always active while available, do not occupy one of the six loadout slots, and cannot be disabled.
- The local hero's Rage starts at 5 with a fixed maximum of 10, never passively replenishes, and decays immediately at 1 Rage per second while no attack or incoming-damage interaction occurs. Every attack grants 3 Rage at launch, actual incoming damage grants 2 Rage, and a successful block or dodge grants 1 Rage. Rage is capped at 10 and never goes negative.
- Melee hero attacks use visible wind-up areas and damage only targets still overlapping when the area resolves.
- Ranged hero attacks use projectiles and damage only on projectile collision. Staff attacks are magical; one-handed throwing-axe attacks and Rending Throw are physical, target only within their configured short range, and use the same single-hit projectile collision rules.

## Damage Sources

- Enemy body contact does not damage the hero.
- Ranged enemy body contact never deals damage.
- Hero health is reduced only by resolved enemy melee attack areas, hostile projectile collisions, and status effects.
- Before health loss, equipped-item Dodge is added to attribute Dodge and capped at 50%. A requirement-active immunity prevents its matching Physical, Magic/Electric, Fire, Frost/Cold, Poison, or Bleed damage entirely. Otherwise Defense subtracts flat damage, and the matching resistance reduces the remainder, capped at 50%.
- Enemy health is reduced only by resolved hero melee attack areas, hero projectile collisions, and status effects.
- A unit's own attack area or projectile never damages that unit.
- Damage areas and projectiles use circle or area overlap checks against unit collision radii.
- Public balance-profile multipliers are applied once at the combat calculation boundary. Development and production currently use identical combat multipliers so local play does not indirectly accelerate durable progression.
- Every hostile damage event, including deterministic one-second status ticks, may be blocked by an equipped buckler while its reactive Blocking passive is ready. Chance is the requirement-adjusted buckler/attribute chance plus `0.01 * effective Blocking level`, capped at 100%; the level contribution is not reduced by unmet buckler requirements. A successful block starts its cooldown; damage received during that cooldown cannot block. Dexterity references mean Agility.
- Direct weapon and skill hits first roll dodge at `min(35%, 0.3% * Agility)`. Status ticks, reflection, and self-paid life costs cannot dodge. A dodge prevents damage and all on-hit effects. Successful dodges and blocks emit `DODGE` and `BLOCK` floating text from the affected hero or enemy; a partial block also emits its remaining damage number. When the local hero dodges or successfully blocks an incoming hit, it gains 1 Rage; any other hit that actually removes hero HP grants 2 Rage. These gains are capped at maximum Rage and do not stack a second damage gain for the same blocked or dodged hit.
- Block chance is `min(100%, 10% * rarityPower + 0.5% * (Strength + Agility))`. A block can occur only when the defender has its full block cost available. Each successful block immediately spends that cost; failed rolls spend nothing. Block cost has a hard floor of 1 rage. A buckler without percentage return costs 1; a buckler with Return costs `1 + ((15% + 0.4% * Agility) * rarityPower) / (1 + 0.1 * itemLevel)`, so stronger percentage return costs more while upgrading the buckler moves the premium back toward the floor. Success prevents `min(incomingDamage, Strength)`.
- Spiked bucklers reflect the rarity-scaled sum of rolled components: `1`, `0.2 * Strength`, and `incomingDamage * (15% + 0.4% * Agility)`. Reflection may be blocked but cannot reflect again, critically strike, apply affixes, or create statuses.
- Damage and statuses retain source attribution for realm-kill credit.
- A direct hero or enemy weapon hit may trigger equipped life steal after mitigation and health clamping. It uses health actually removed, cannot recursively trigger itself, and does not apply to reflected or periodic status damage.
- Resolved damage, healing, dodges, and blocks create short-lived floating canvas text that rises and fades from the affected unit. A damage number shows the rolled damage remaining after block/mitigation but before clamping against the target's remaining HP, so overkill still communicates weapon output; healing shows only health actually restored. Physical damage is light gray; critical hits override the base color with yellow-white; magic is yellow; electric is cyan; poison is pink-purple; fire is red-orange; bleed is red; healing is green; dodge is pale cyan; and block is shield gold. Shield reflection uses the triggering damage type's color, cannot be styled as a critical hit, and remains visible even when the damaged unit is removed in the same simulation update.

## Enemy Attacks

- Melee creeps pursue the hero until their attack range is reached.
- When a melee creep starts a swing, it immediately telegraphs a circular attack area.
- A melee attack area resolves after a wind-up duration derived from the attacker's attack speed.
- If a creep dies during that wind-up, its unresolved melee attack area is canceled immediately and deals no damage.
- The hero takes melee damage only if still inside the telegraphed area when it resolves.
- Enemies with ranged weapons retreat below their authored minimum distance, stop elsewhere within attack range, and approach only from beyond attack range. They telegraph their shot, then launch a projectile toward the hero's position at firing time. Staff users retain the bubble presentation; throwing-axe users launch physical axes and engage at 4.2 m.
- Enemy projectiles travel independently and deal damage only on circle collision with the hero. Their weapon on-hit effects, including throwing-axe bleed, resolve on collision.
- A projectile that has already launched remains active if its source creep dies.
- Orbiting Hammer projectiles capture the caster's position at cast time and remain centered on that fixed world point, regardless of later caster movement or defeat. Their angle advances continuously and their orbit radius expands from 0.56 to 3.8 m over their first 2.4 seconds, then remains at that radius. Three are emitted per cast at evenly spaced starting angles, with a small deterministic per-hammer/per-cast angular drift so successive paths do not stack exactly. Each uses a compact low-poly 3D hammer model centered above the arena plane and visibly tumbles around all three local axes while orbiting; a camera-facing hammer silhouette remains available while the model loads or after a load failure. A hammer remains active after collision for its full level-scaled lifetime, may damage multiple enemies, and remembers hit unit ids so it can damage each enemy at most once.
- Bubble projectiles can be dodged and expire after their lifetime, a unit hit, Force Field cancellation, or a column collision; arena margins do not remove them.
- Attack wind-up and recovery slow creep movement.

## Telegraph Rendering

- Every area-based attack is shown clearly before it resolves.
- Resolved attack areas briefly flash after damage resolution.
- Hero attack areas use the hero combat color, and enemy attack areas use the enemy threat color.
- The HUD and arena should show the hero's facing or auto-aim direction, equipped-weapon attacks, drops, and inspected enemy highlight.
- Weapon-skill casts create deterministic, short-lived canvas effects without consuming combat randomness: Club Bash uses an expanding impact ring and debris, Sword Sweep a directional crescent, Dagger Flurry crossing blade streaks, Mace Shockwave concentric rings and radial sparks, Staff Arcane Bolt an arcane launch burst followed on impact by a radius-matched expanding explosion with particles and a bright fading blue point light, and Healing a subdued one-second horizontal green ground aura with an extremely strong matching green point light at `z = 6`, slightly above ground level, lingering for one additional second plus separate camera-facing plus-sign sprites rising along world Z whose timing follows `specs/spells/healing.md`. Spell lights are hero-only renderer resources rather than children of transient effects: an available hero skill prewarms one cached light for each illuminated effect kind, inactive cached lights remain at zero intensity, casts only move and retime them, and unequipping removes them from the scene without discarding the cache. Multiple simultaneous effects of one kind share that single light and the latest effect wins, so simultaneous Arcane Bolt explosions may intentionally show only one flash. Enemy spell effects never drive these lights. Visual geometry remains presentation-only arena objects, updates in the fixed loop, and is removed at the end of its bounded lifetime.
- Fire Breath renders as a short forward-moving series of red-orange arcs. Its cone resolves once after the telegraph and applies Burn for 24 one-second ticks. Burn uses fire-colored combat text and cannot itself dodge.
- Gooey Swamp is a persistent, stationary ground-zone spell defined in `specs/spells/swamp.md`. Ground zones update before creeps choose their movement for the fixed step, apply their movement multiplier only while a creep overlaps the zone, and render beneath all creeps.

## Local Defeat Reset

- Player health reaching zero starts a 1.8-second, non-interactive full-screen defeat modal in the restrained style of a dark fantasy death screen. It shows large red `YOU DIED` text with the smaller white line `Your legacy inherited your remaining resources and spellbooks and will try to avenge you…` beneath it. The former `Hero down` wave-banner notification is not shown.
- When the defeat notice finishes, the client clears active creeps, attack areas, projectiles, drops, and pending local wave spawns.
- The hero is reset to the arena center with progression-derived resources.
- Score, permanent progression, inventory, canonical username identity, and server-owned wave number are not reset by the local arena reset.
- A server-authored wave marks entry into a newly requested solo realm or a newly matched competitive realm. Before that wave is queued, the client clears the previous arena, restores the hero to full health, mana, and rage, removes every bleed, poison, burn, stun, shock, freeze, and curse instance, clears reactive/block and temporary-surge state, stops residual velocity, and resets hero attack and skill cooldowns. Ordinary later wave dispatches inside the same realm do not restore resources or statuses.
- Defeat is reported to the server. Server-owned wave number adjustment and replacement wave dispatch are defined in `specs/SPEC.md`.
- In Training Grounds health is clamped to at least 1 and defeat never starts.

## Exact Simulation Constants and Order

- For an unlearned active spell supplied by the equipped weapon, its attack-trigger chance is multiplied by the number of units the triggering weapon attack hits and capped at 100%; a three-unit hit therefore has three times the single-target trigger chance.

- Run fixed updates at `1/60` second. In each update: release due spawns and synchronize build/passive inputs; capture the active unit and effect lists; compile every unit's authoritative state from clean defaults; update unit movement and action creation; update spells, particles, attack areas, projectiles, ground zones, and collisions; resolve damage, healing, and newly applied effects; advance effect lifecycles and expire completed effects; correct boundaries; report newly dead units and overlapping drops; update combat text; remove inactive objects; synchronize the HUD; handle defeat; then update the camera. Render after all available fixed updates. Conditional auras and ground modifiers are collected from the positions at compilation time, so entering one during movement affects the next fixed update. Effects created during an update likewise begin contributing on the next fixed update.
- Hero radius is 0.36 m, base movement speed is 4.7 m/s, and acceleration is 18.4 m/s²; active attack areas reduce maximum movement speed to 48%. Regular creep radius is 0.32 m, rival radius 0.44 m. Melee and bubble-shooter movement is 1.44 m/s with 3.8 m/s² acceleration; rival movement is 2 m/s with 5 m/s² acceleration. Agility multiplies enemy maximum speed by `1 + 0.01 * Agility`. Training multiplies creep speed by 0.5.
- Melee range is 62. Melee creeps stop at or below 62 and approach above 62. Bubble shooters attack at 330, stand still from 210 through 330, retreat below 210, and approach above 330. Other ranged creeps use the same rule with their authored attack range and retreat threshold. Initial creep cooldown is uniformly `0.5 + random * 0.4` seconds. Melee wind-up is `0.7 / attacksPerSecond` and recovery is `0.75 / attacksPerSecond`; ranged wind-up is `0.65 / attacksPerSecond` and recovery is `1.15 / attacksPerSecond`. Wind-up steering uses one quarter maximum speed.
- Enemy melee areas have range 70, a full-circle arc, their computed wind-up, and 0.14 seconds resolved linger. Hero melee areas wind up for 0.18 and linger 0.13 seconds. Their attack sectors are full circle for Bash, Sweep, Shockwave, and unskilled club/mace/hammer attacks; linearly scale from 45 degrees at effective level 1 to 270 degrees at effective level 99 for Cleave; use 2.2 radians for Flurry; and use 1.44 radians otherwise. Runtime attack areas store half of these authored sector angles.
- Standard projectiles have a 0.22 m radius, move at 4.9 m/s, and live 4 seconds without an arena-boundary limit. Vampiric Boomerang is three times as broad at a 0.66 m radius, travels both legs at 3.6 m/s, and lies flat facing world-up instead of billboarding toward the camera. It samples collision every 0.5 seconds while active and may resolve every eligible overlap in a sample. Each overlapping enemy takes 0.5 seconds of the Boomerang's cast damage per second and receives half the normal linear projectile impact force. Orbiting Hammers live `2.4 + 7.6 * (skillLevel - 1) / 98` seconds (2.4 seconds at level 1, 10 seconds at level 99), rotate at 5.2 radians/second, and expand linearly from a 0.56 m to 3.8 m radius during their first 2.4 seconds. Frozen Orb moves at 1.5 m/s for 4 seconds, emits eight radial single-hit spikes every 0.45 seconds, and does not disappear when its orb overlaps an enemy; its spikes travel at 4.7 m/s for 1.2 seconds. Other projectiles are single-hit.
- A basic or non-Flurry skilled attack recovers in `1 / attacksPerSecond`; Flurry uses `0.35 / attacksPerSecond`. Physical skills add 0.35 rage to the weapon's normal cost. Most magical skills cost 1 mana; Healing costs `5 + 2 * effective skill level + 0.25 * HP actually restored`, Orbiting Hammers costs 6, Rapid Regeneration costs 4, Force Field costs 8, and Frozen Orb costs 10. Force Field always pushes outward regardless of the main hand. Reflective Surge costs 3 rage. Healing auto-casts at or below 50% HP and restores a level-scaled 40%–100% fraction of current HP plus `5% * maxHP` and `5% * maxHP * currentRage / maxRage`. Its effective radius scales linearly from 3 m at level 1 to 12 m at level 99. A creep with Healing automatically casts under the same HP and mana gates with its independent 18-to-6-second level cooldown; it heals itself and every allied active creep within that radius, creating green floating healing text on affected creeps and one caster-centered green aura and rising-plus effect at the same radius. Rapid Regeneration is a self-buff that only casts while its caster is missing health and has no active instance; it multiplies all normal/equipment health regeneration and adds its flat HP/second value for its level-scaled duration.
- Standard melee attacks and their physical skills use radial force; Cleave multiplies the emitted baseline radial impulse by 2. Standard projectile attacks, Rending Throw, Arcane Bolt, and Frozen Orb spikes use linear force. Force Field is a special radial velocity replacement. Fire Breath, Gooey Swamp, Sunburn, poison, burn, bleed, electric auras, reflection, and every other periodic or passive damage source have no force. Status procs roll independently on a direct weapon hit. Throwing axes have a base 15% bleed chance before compatible affixes are added, and Rending Throw guarantees one standard bleed in addition to independently rolled procs. All Poison, Bleed, and Burn application durations are three times their former values for every source and target. Standard Bleed lasts 9 seconds at 0.25 damage/second; Poison lasts 24 seconds at `0.2 + 0.02 * targetSpirit` damage/second; stun lasts 0.7 seconds; and each Frost stack lasts 4 seconds. A creep's Frost threshold is `round(3 + 12 * min(1, Frost resistance))`, ranging from 3 stacks at 0% resistance to 15 at 100%. Below the threshold, each active Frost stack removes `1 / threshold` of that creep's movement speed. Reaching the threshold activates Freeze: the creep stops steering and retains only frictionless velocity from impacts or Force Field, sliding like an icicle until its active Frost stack count drops below the threshold. The threshold-reaching stack cancels existing self-propelled velocity before the applying hit's force resolves. Frost immunity prevents Frost stacks entirely. Applying a Frost stack removes one active Burn stack, and applying Burn removes one active Frost stack, one-for-one before its own stack is added; Fire immunity prevents Burn, while Poison and Bleed immunity prevent their respective statuses. Bash always stuns 1.1 seconds, Shockwave 0.6, Sweep bleeds for 9 seconds at 0.35/second, and Cleave bleeds for 6 seconds at 0.45/second. Unique Arcane Bolt explosions apply a 4-second Freeze throughout their doubled radius; ordinary Arcane Bolt explosions do not apply Freeze. Frozen Orb and each emitted spike apply one Frost stack. Status instances stack as separate records, periodic instances tick once per second, and any active stun prevents steering.
- While Reflective Surge is active, the affected hero or creep replaces its usual textured appearance with one uniform dark-gray metallic material. This override lasts exactly as long as the buff, takes precedence over ordinary status tinting, and restores every original texture and material property when the buff expires; the one-render-frame white hit flash may still appear on top.
- The full-screen death-modal presentation lasts 1.8 seconds before local reset and replacement-wave request. Hover selection accepts the nearest active creep only within its radius plus 0.16 m. Drop pickup uses circle overlap and records one pending request only when its WebSocket send succeeds. After a rejected request it suppresses retries until the hero leaves that drop's overlap. A bounded acknowledgement timeout or connection recovery triggers the authoritative Gold, Scrap, and equipment reconciliation defined by `specs/PROGRESSION_SPEC.md`; reconciliation clears resolved pending ids, removes client-only drops, and places reissued server-only drops at the hero's current position.
